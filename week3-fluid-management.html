Fluid Management

Content Status: This page covers assessment and management of fluid volume imbalances — hypovolemia, hypervolemia, and third spacing — with nursing interventions and monitoring strategies.

Fluid Compartments & Osmolality

  • Total body water: ~60% of body weight (lower in elderly and obese patients).
  • Intracellular fluid (ICF): 2/3 of total water (inside cells) — regulated by potassium.
  • Extracellular fluid (ECF): 1/3 of total water. Contains plasma (in blood vessels) and interstitial fluid (around cells).
  • Osmolality: Concentration of dissolved particles. Normal 280–300 mOsm/kg. Controls fluid movement between compartments.

Hypovolemia (Dehydration)

SIGNS: ↓ BP, ↑ HR, ↑ RR, dry mucous membranes, poor skin turgor, ↓ urine output. LABS: ↑ BUN/Cr ratio, ↑ osmolality, ↑ sodium, ↓ urine sodium.

Hypervolemia (Fluid Overload)

SIGNS: Edema (peripheral/sacral), weight gain, rales/crackles, JVD, HTN, dyspnea. CAUSE: Heart failure, renal failure, cirrhosis, excessive IV fluids, hypoalbuminemia.
  • Pulmonary edema risk: CVP >8 cm H₂O or elevated PCWP. Signs: orthopnea, PND, pink frothy sputum.
  • Nursing management: Fluid restriction (500–1000 mL/day), diuretics, sodium restriction, head of bed elevated, I&Os, daily weights.
  • Monitor: Lung sounds, JVD, edema, BP, weight trends. Report sudden weight gain (>2–3 lbs/day).

Third Spacing

  • Definition: Fluid shifts from intravascular space into interstitial space but is not available for cellular use. Patient appears dehydrated inside but edematous outside.
  • Causes: Burns, sepsis, peritonitis, liver cirrhosis (ascites), nephrotic syndrome, severe malnutrition.
  • Problem: Standard fluid replacement calculations underestimate needs. Monitor urine output, CVP, and weight carefully.
  • Reabsorption phase: After initial injury heals, fluid reabsorbs into vasculature. Risk of hypervolemia and hyperkalemia (from cell necrosis).

Daily Maintenance & Replacement

  • Maintenance fluids: ~30 mL/kg/day for average 70 kg adult = ~2100 mL/day ≈ 1 mL per kcal burned.
  • Replace measured losses: GI (diarrhea, NG), urine (polyuria, diuretics), insensible (sweating, fever, hyperventilation).
  • Fever rule: Add 500 mL for each 1°C above 37°C.
  • Monitor: Urine specific gravity (target 1.010–1.025), urine output (goal 0.5–1 mL/kg/hr), daily weights, I&Os.

Study Flashcards

A patient loses 1.5 L of diarrhea. What degree of dehydration?
Moderate (10%). Expected findings: tachycardia, postural hypotension. Treat with IV isotonic fluids.
What is third spacing? Give one example.
Fluid shifts from intravascular to interstitial space and is trapped there. Example: burns, sepsis, peritonitis causing edema despite patient being intravascularly depleted.
Why is D5W problematic in certain patients?
After dextrose is metabolized, D5W becomes hypotonic free water. In elderly, post-op, or SIADH patients, continuous D5W causes dilutional hyponatremia.
Calculate maintenance fluids for a 80 kg patient.
80 kg × 30 mL/kg = 2400 mL/day approximately. Adjust for insensible losses and measured output.
