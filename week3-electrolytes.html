Electrolyte Imbalances: Comprehensive Study Guide

Sodium, Potassium, Calcium, Magnesium, and Phosphate

📌 Critical Concept: Electrolytes & Cellular Function

Electrolytes maintain cell osmolarity, nerve conduction, muscle contraction, and cardiac function.

Normal range varies by electrolyte. Imbalances cause symptoms INSIDE cells (confusion, weakness) or in organ systems (arrhythmias, seizures).

Key Principle: Don't memorize individual symptoms - think CELLULAR DYSFUNCTION. Hypo = cell swells; Hyper = cell shrivels. Both cause problems.

🧂 SODIUM (Na+): The Osmolarity Boss

Normal: 135-145 mEq/L | Controls total body water osmolarity
Remember: Na+ controls where water goes. ↓Na = water enters cells (swelling); ↑Na = water leaves cells (shrinkage)

HYPONATREMIA (Na+ <135)

Causes:

  • Dilution: SIADH (syndrome of inappropriate ADH), excessive free water intake, IV hypotonic fluids
  • Depletion: Vomiting, diarrhea, diuretics, adrenal insufficiency, burns
  • Na loss > water loss: Net hyponatremia

Pathophysiology:

Low Na → hypotonic solution → water enters cells → CEREBRAL EDEMA (most dangerous)

Signs & Symptoms (Brain + GI Focus):

  • MILD (120-130): Confusion, headache, anorexia, nausea
  • MODERATE (110-120): Restlessness, irritability, muscle cramps
  • SEVERE (<110) - EMERGENCY: Seizures, coma, cerebral edema, respiratory depression, DEATH
⚠️ CRITICAL: Seizures from hyponatremia are HYPO-OSMOLAR seizures (brain swelling)
Correct Na+ SLOWLY (8-12 mEq/L in 24h) to avoid osmotic shrinkage death (ODS - osmotic demyelination syndrome)

Nursing Actions:

  • Monitor neuro status closely (seizure precautions if severe)
  • 3% hypertonic saline SLOWLY if symptomatic (seizures, coma)
  • Monitor Na+ frequently (every 2-4 hours initially)
  • Fluid restriction if SIADH
  • Treat underlying cause

HYPERNATREMIA (Na+ >145)

Causes:

  • Water loss: Diabetes insipidus, excessive sweating, hyperventilation, osmotic diuresis
  • Na+ gain: Hypertonic saline, sea water ingestion
  • Inadequate free water intake: Dehydration, no access to water

Pathophysiology:

High Na → hypertonic solution → water leaves cells → CELLULAR SHRINKAGE (including brain)

Signs & Symptoms (Brain + Thirst Focus):

  • Extreme thirst (unless altered mental status)
  • Restlessness, agitation, confusion
  • Muscle weakness, lethargy
  • SEVERE: Seizures (HYPER-osmolar type - different mechanism), coma, intracranial bleeding from brain cell shrinkage
⚠️ Brain shrinkage can cause intracranial bleeding! Correct SLOWLY to avoid swelling rebound

Nursing Actions:

  • Hypotonic fluids (D5W, 0.45% NS) SLOWLY
  • Free water by mouth if able, or NG tube
  • Correct Na+ NO faster than 8-10 mEq/L in 24h
  • Monitor neuro status, seizure precautions
  • Check for diabetes insipidus if recurrent

⚡ POTASSIUM (K+): The Cardiac Boss

Normal: 3.5-5.0 mEq/L | Controls cardiac electrical activity and muscle contraction
Most critical electrolyte for cardiac function. MINOR changes = MAJOR cardiac effects.

HYPOKALEMIA (K+ <3.5)

Causes:

  • Urinary losses: Diuretics (especially thiazides and loops), aldosteronism, renal tubular acidosis
  • GI losses: Vomiting, diarrhea (most common!), NG suctioning
  • Intracellular shift: Insulin, alkalosis, sympathomimetics
  • Dietary: Inadequate K+ intake

Pathophysiology:

Low K → hyperpolarized (more negative) resting membrane potential → muscle & heart won't depolarize well

Signs & Symptoms (Muscle + Cardiac Focus):

  • Muscle: Weakness, fatigue, leg cramps, rhabdomyolysis (if severe)
  • Cardiac: Palpitations, arrhythmias (premature beats, atrial fibrillation)
  • GI: Nausea, vomiting, constipation, ileus (K+ needed for GI motility)
  • Neuro: Confusion, irritability
EKG Changes in Hypokalemia: Flat T wave, ST depression, U wave prominence
⚠️ Most dangerous in patients on digoxin (increases digitalis toxicity risk)

Nursing Actions:

  • Cardiac monitoring if K+ <3.0
  • PO K+ supplement if able (banana, orange juice, slow-release tablets)
  • IV K+ if symptomatic or severe (20-40 mEq in 500mL saline, infuse through central line if possible - irritating to veins)
  • NEVER give K+ as rapid bolus - causes fatal hyperkalemia
  • Treat underlying cause (replace diarrheal losses, reduce diuretic if possible)
  • Monitor EKG, serum K+ frequently

HYPERKALEMIA (K+ >5.0)

Causes:

  • Decreased urinary excretion: Renal failure, ACE inhibitors, K-sparing diuretics, aldosterone deficiency
  • Cellular release: Cell death (trauma, burns, rhabdo), hemolysis, tumor lysis, acidosis
  • Intake: Excessive K+ supplementation, salt substitutes

Pathophysiology:

High K → depolarized resting membrane potential → irritable, fires too easily = arrhythmias

Signs & Symptoms (Cardiac + Muscle Focus):

  • Often ASYMPTOMATIC until severe
  • Cardiac: Palpitations, chest pain, syncope, DANGEROUS ARRHYTHMIAS (peaked T wave, prolonged QRS, loss of P wave)
  • Muscle: Weakness (ascending paralysis in severe cases), paresthesias
  • GI: Nausea, diarrhea
⚠️ CARDIAC EMERGENCY: K+ >6.5 with EKG changes = IMMEDIATE TREATMENT
Peaked T wave → widened QRS → loss of P wave → sine wave → VENTRICULAR FIBRILLATION/ARREST

STAT Nursing Interventions (Remember "CIKE"):

  • Calcium gluconate 10% IV (PROTECTS cardiac membrane from arrhythmias) - give FIRST for EKG changes
  • Insulin with glucose (drives K+ into cells): Regular insulin 10 units IV + 50mL D50W
  • Katecholamines/beta-agonists: Albuterol 10-20mg nebulized (drives K+ intracellular)
  • Excretion: Loop diuretics, cation exchange resin (sodium polystyrene = Kayexalate PO/PR), dialysis if renal failure

Also:

  • Limit K+ intake
  • Treat underlying cause (hold ACE-I, hold K-sparing diuretic)
  • Cardiac monitoring

🦴 CALCIUM (Ca2+): The Neuromuscular Boss

Normal: 8.5-10.5 mg/dL (ionized Ca: 4.5-5.3 mg/dL) | Controls nerve and muscle excitability
Remember: 99% in bones; 1% in blood. Only ionized Ca is biologically active.

HYPOCALCEMIA (Ca2+ <8.5)

Causes:

  • Vitamin D deficiency: Poor intake, no sun exposure, malabsorption
  • Kidney disease: Can't activate vitamin D
  • Parathyroid: Hypoparathyroidism (PTH deficiency)
  • Protein loss: Albumin carries calcium; low albumin = low total Ca
  • Phosphate excess: Causes Ca × PO4 precipitation
  • Pancreatitis, rhabdomyolysis: Consumes calcium

Pathophysiology:

Low Ca → neurons & muscle more excitable → tetany, seizures, arrhythmias

Signs & Symptoms (TETANY Classic):

  • Paresthesias: Tingling lips, fingers, toes (EARLY sign)
  • Muscle cramps, spasms
  • Tetany: Involuntary muscle contraction (hand/wrist flexion - "carpopedal spasm")
  • Positive Trousseau sign: Inflate BP cuff on arm → hand goes into tetany (classic exam finding)
  • Positive Chvostek sign: Tap cheek → facial muscles twitch
  • Cardiac: Prolonged QT interval, arrhythmias
  • Seizures if severe

Nursing Actions:

  • Seizure precautions if symptomatic
  • Calcium supplementation: PO (calcium carbonate with vitamin D) or IV (calcium gluconate)
  • IV calcium SLOWLY (through central line if possible - tissue necrosis if extravasation)
  • Vitamin D supplementation
  • Treat underlying cause
  • Ionized calcium more accurate than total calcium (especially if low albumin)

HYPERCALCEMIA (Ca2+ >10.5)

Causes:

  • Hyperparathyroidism: Most common cause
  • Malignancy: PTHrP secretion (lung cancer), osteolytic lesions (multiple myeloma), calcitriol production (lymphomas)
  • Vitamin D excess: Supplements, granulomatous diseases (sarcoidosis, TB)
  • Vitamin A toxicity
  • Hyperthyroidism, immobility

Pathophysiology:

High Ca → neurons & muscle LESS excitable → weakness, lethargy, arrhythmias

Signs & Symptoms ("Stones, Bones, Groans, Psych"):

  • Nephrolithiasis ("Stones"): Kidney stones
  • Bone: Bone pain, osteoporosis
  • Groans: GI - nausea, vomiting, constipation, anorexia
  • Psychiatric ("Psych"): Confusion, depression, psychosis, "hypercalcemic stupor"
  • Cardiac: Shortened QT, arrhythmias
  • Neuro: Weakness, lethargy, headache
⚠️ Remember mnemonic: "CHIMPANZEES" for hypercalcemia = Confusion, Hypertension, Insomnia, Muscle weakness, Polyuria, Anorexia, Nausea, Zestlessness, Eructation, EKG changes

Nursing Actions:

  • Aggressive IV hydration (saline to dilute, increase renal excretion)
  • Loop diuretics (after hydration) to increase urinary calcium excretion
  • Bisphosphonates (reduce bone resorption) - takes days to work
  • Calcitonin (if severe, immediate effect) - reduces bone resorption
  • Steroids (if granulomatous disease)
  • Avoid thiazide diuretics (increase calcium)
  • Treat underlying cause

🧲 MAGNESIUM (Mg2+): The Enzyme Cofactor

Normal: 1.7-2.2 mg/dL | Required for ATP, protein synthesis, muscle & nerve function
Often overlooked but CRITICAL for cardiac stability, K+ balance, and Ca2+ regulation

HYPOMAGNESEMIA (Mg2+ <1.7)

Causes:

  • GI losses: Diarrhea (most common), vomiting, malabsorption, short bowel
  • Renal losses: Diuretics, alcoholism, cisplatin, amphotericin B
  • Poor intake: Alcoholism

Signs & Symptoms (Cardiac + Neuro):

  • Cardiac: Arrhythmias (PVCs, SVT, torsades de pointes), ECG changes
  • Neuro: Tremor, personality changes, vertigo
  • Muscle: Tetany, weakness
  • Often ASYMPTOMATIC despite low level
⚠️ Hypomagnesemia makes K+ and Ca2+ difficult to correct - MUST check/correct Mg first!
Refractory hypokalemia or hypocalcemia = think hypomagnesemia

Nursing Actions:

  • Magnesium supplementation: PO or IV depending on severity
  • IV: Magnesium sulfate (MgSO4) 1-2g in 250mL saline, infuse slowly
  • PO: Magnesium oxide (causes diarrhea), magnesium glycinate
  • Check/correct K+ and Ca2+ as well

HYPERMAGNESEMIA (Mg2+ >2.2)

Causes:

  • Renal failure (most common)
  • Excessive intake: Antacids with Mg, laxatives, IV Mg infusion

Signs & Symptoms (Depressant effects):

  • Muscle: Weakness, paralysis
  • Cardiac: Bradycardia, arrhythmias, ECG changes (prolonged PR, widened QRS, tall T wave)
  • Neuro: Lethargy, confusion, depression of reflexes (flaccid paralysis in severe)
  • Respiratory: Respiratory depression (if severe)

Nursing Actions:

  • Restrict dietary/supplement Mg
  • IV calcium gluconate (antagonizes Mg effects on cardiac membrane)
  • Saline diuresis + loop diuretics
  • Dialysis if renal failure

🧬 PHOSPHATE (PO4): The Energy Player

Normal: 2.5-4.5 mg/dL | Essential for ATP, DNA synthesis, and oxygen delivery
Inversely related to calcium (high PO4 = low Ca when multiplied)

HYPOPHOSPHATEMIA (PO4 <2.5)

Causes:

  • Refeeding syndrome: Insulin drives PO4 into cells after starvation
  • Respiratory alkalosis: Hyperventilation
  • GI losses: Antacids, diarrhea
  • Alcoholism

Signs & Symptoms (Muscle + RBC dysfunction):

  • Muscle: Weakness (especially respiratory muscles), rhabdomyolysis
  • Neuro: Confusion, seizures, coma
  • Cardiac: Arrhythmias, cardiomyopathy
  • Hemolysis: RBCs need PO4 for ATP - become rigid
  • Often asymptomatic until severe

Nursing Actions:

  • Phosphate supplementation: IV or PO
  • Careful with refeeding (high-carb feeds drive PO4 intracellular)
  • Monitor for respiratory failure (weakened respiratory muscles)

HYPERPHOSPHATEMIA (PO4 >4.5)

Causes:

  • Renal failure (most common - kidney can't excrete PO4)
  • Cell destruction: Tumor lysis syndrome, rhabdomyolysis, hemolysis
  • Excessive intake: Phosphate-containing laxatives

Pathophysiology:

High PO4 + normal Ca = Ca × PO4 precipitation in tissues (soft tissue calcification, secondary hyperparathyroidism)

Signs & Symptoms:

  • Metastatic calcification: Arthralgias, soft tissue hardening
  • Secondary effects: From resulting hypocalcemia (tetany, seizures)

Nursing Actions:

  • Restrict dietary phosphate
  • Phosphate binders (aluminum hydroxide, sevelamer, calcium-based)
  • Treat renal disease / control PTH

📊 Quick Reference: Electrolyte Imbalances at a Glance

Electrolyte Normal Range Hypo Signs Hyper Signs Priority Intervention
Na+ 135-145 Confusion, seizures, cerebral edema Thirst, seizures, intracranial bleed Correct SLOWLY (8-12 mEq/24h)
K+ 3.5-5.0 Weakness, EKG changes, arrhythmias Cardiac emergency, peaked T wave Hypo: PO supplement; Hyper: CIKE protocol
Ca2+ 8.5-10.5 Tetany, Trousseau/Chvostek signs Stones, bones, groans, psych Hypo: IV Ca gluconate; Hyper: hydration + diuretics
Mg2+ 1.7-2.2 Arrhythmias (refractory to K/Ca correction) Weakness, paralysis, respiratory depression Hypo: supplementation; Hyper: saline diuresis
PO4 2.5-4.5 Muscle weakness, hemolysis Soft tissue calcification Hypo: supplementation; Hyper: phosphate binders

📝 Key Takeaways

  • ✓ Hyponatremia seizures = SLOW correction (8-12 mEq/24h) to avoid osmotic demyelination
  • ✓ Hyperkalemia cardiac changes: Peaked T → widened QRS → loss P wave → sine wave → arrest
  • ✓ Hyperkalemia CIKE protocol: Calcium (cardiac protect), Insulin+glucose, Kayexalate/K-agonists, Excretion
  • ✓ Hypocalcemia tetany = Trousseau & Chvostek signs (positive = inflated cuff causes hand spasm)
  • ✓ Hypercalcemia "Stones, Bones, Groans, Psych"
  • ✓ Refractory hypokalemia or hypocalcemia = check magnesium FIRST
  • ✓ Ca × PO4 product: hyperphosphatemia causes metastatic calcification
  • ✓ Ionized calcium more accurate when protein (albumin) low
  • ✓ EKG changes: Each electrolyte has specific changes (flat T for K↓, peaked T for K↑, shortened QT for Ca↑)
  • ✓ Cardiac monitoring for: hypo/hyperkalemia, hypo/hypercalcemia, hypomagnesemia
