Preoperative Nursing

Content Status: This page covers preoperative nursing responsibilities, patient assessment, teaching, and preparation. Includes informed consent, medication management, NPO status, and anxiety reduction.

Preoperative Timeline & Assessment

  • Early preoperative phase: When surgery is scheduled until admission to hospital/surgical facility (days to weeks).
  • Immediate preoperative phase: From admission until patient enters OR (hours).
  • Initial assessment: Medical/surgical history, medications (especially anticoagulants, antiplatelets), allergies, baseline vitals, lab values, NPO status verification.

Informed Consent

CONSENT REQUIREMENTS: (1) Patient must understand procedure, risks, benefits, alternatives. (2) Voluntary — no coercion. (3) Competent — mentally able to decide. (4) Informed — physician explains; nurse verifies understanding. (5) Documented — signed consent form in chart.
  • Nurse's role: Verify consent is signed BEFORE premedication. If patient questions or appears confused, notify provider before surgery proceeds.
  • Special situations: Emergency (verbal consent accepted), minors (parent/guardian consents), incompetent patients (proxy decision-maker).
  • Do NOT witness consent if you explained the procedure — provider must explain and nurse can only verify understanding.

Preoperative Teaching

  • Deep breathing & coughing: Prevent postop atelectasis and pneumonia. Teach splinting incision with pillow to reduce pain during cough.
  • Leg exercises & mobility: Flex/extend legs, circle ankles, walk early post-op to prevent DVT/PE. Contraindicated only in specific surgeries (e.g. some orthopedic).
  • Pain management: Explain PCA pump, medications available, pain scale. Reassure that pain medication will be provided post-op.
  • Sensory information: Describe what to expect (OR lights, monitors, cold room), sounds, recovery room experience.

Preoperative Checklist

  • ☐ Signed surgical consent in chart
  • ☐ Signed anesthesia consent
  • ☐ H&P documented
  • ☐ Labs (CBC, BMP, type & screen/cross) reviewed
  • ☐ EKG/CXR if indicated and reviewed
  • ☐ NPO verified (time documented)
  • ☐ Void before premedication
  • ☐ Jewelry, dentures, contacts, makeup, nail polish removed
  • ☐ ID band, allergy band secure
  • ☐ Premedication given (if ordered)
  • ☐ Side rails up after premedication
  • ☐ Call light within reach, family orientated

Anxiety Reduction & Psychological Support

  • Common preop fears: pain, death, loss of control, unknown outcome, body image changes.
  • Nursing interventions: Active listening, provide factual information, encourage verbalization of concerns, presence of family, relaxation techniques, spiritual support if requested.
  • Premedication (sedatives like midazolam) given to reduce anxiety — monitor for respiratory depression.

Study Flashcards

A patient states, "I don't really understand why I need this surgery." What should you do?
Do NOT proceed. Notify the surgeon immediately. Patient does not have informed consent. Surgeon must re-explain until patient demonstrates understanding.
How long before surgery must a patient be NPO for solid food?
Typically 6 hours for solid food; 2 hours for clear liquids (facility protocol may vary). Verify with anesthesia.
Why is deep breathing & coughing important preoperatively?
Post-op atelectasis (collapsed alveoli) can lead to pneumonia. Teaching before surgery allows practice and builds confidence. Splint incision to reduce pain during cough.
Can hearing aids stay in during surgery if patient is NOT being intubated?
Yes, in some cases. They help the patient communicate in the OR. However, verify with anesthesia — some facilities require removal for safety.
