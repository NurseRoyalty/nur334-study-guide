Intraoperative Nursing

Content Status: This page covers the intraoperative period — roles of the surgical team, anesthesia, positioning, monitoring, asepsis, safety considerations, and documentation during surgery.

Intraoperative Nursing Roles

  • Circulating nurse: Remains outside sterile field. Manages supplies, documents, monitors patient safety, communicates with team, assists with patient positioning and draping.
  • Scrub nurse: Works within sterile field. Prepares instruments, passes instruments to surgeon, counts sponges/needles, maintains sterile technique.
  • Anesthesia provider: Monitors vital signs, airway management, fluid/medication administration, pain and sedation levels.
  • Surgeon & assistants: Perform the procedure.

Patient Positioning & Safety

  • Supine: Most common. Risk: pressure on sacrum, heels, back of head.
  • Prone: Face-down. Risk: facial edema, corneal abrasion, brachial plexus injury if arms abducted >90°.
  • Lithotomy: Legs in stirrups. Risk: thrombosis, rhabdomyolysis, compartment syndrome if legs positioned too high/long.
  • Trendelenburg: Head-down. Risk: increased ICP, cerebral edema, pulmonary edema.
  • Lateral: Side-lying. Risk: dependent shoulder/hip pressure, brachial plexus stretch.
  • Positioning rules: Pad all pressure points, avoid extreme joint angles, apply sequential compression devices if prolonged, monitor dependent limbs for color/temperature changes.

Asepsis & Infection Control

STERILE FIELD BOUNDARIES: 1 inch around sterile table is NOT sterile (gravity pulls). Items below waist level are NOT sterile. Only sterile items contact sterile field. Avoid reaching over sterile field.
  • Surgical site prep: Chlorhexidine or iodine-based solution in concentric circles from surgical site outward (one-way sweep). Allow to dry completely.
  • Draping: Sterile drapes isolate surgical site. Cuffed drapes protect sterile gloves from skin edges.
  • Counts: Sponge, instrument, and needle counts performed at opening, during closure, and at end of surgery. Mismatch requires X-ray to rule out retained foreign body.
  • Specimen handling: All specimens labeled with patient ID, type, and site. Placed in formalin or appropriate preservative.

Fluid & Blood Management

  • Fluid therapy: IV fluids (crystalloid or colloid) replace maintenance, deficits, and third-spacing losses. Anesthesia provider manages.
  • Blood loss estimation: Weigh sponges (1 g ≈ 1 mL), measure in suction canisters. Report significant loss to anesthesia/surgeon.
  • Massive transfusion protocol: When >10 units PRBC in 24 hrs, follow ratio of components (FFP, platelets, cryo) to reduce coagulopathy.
  • Cell saver: Autologous blood recovery device for patients with significant blood loss. Reduces transfusion needs.

Temperature Management & Complications

  • Hypothermia (<36°C): Common during general anesthesia due to heat loss, vasodilation, decreased metabolism. Risks: coagulopathy, increased infection, delayed recovery.
  • Prevention: Active warming (forced-air blanket, IV warmer), minimize skin exposure, warm irrigation fluids.
  • Malignant hyperthermia: RARE but FATAL reaction to certain anesthetic agents (succinylcholine, volatile anesthetics). Signs: masseter rigidity, ↑ CO₂, tachycardia, arrhythmias, muscle rigidity, rhabdomyolysis.
  • MH treatment: STOP offending agents immediately, hyperventilate with 100% O₂, DANTROLENE IV (2.5 mg/kg repeat q5min until signs resolve), active cooling, monitor for DIC/hyperkalemia.

Study Flashcards

What is the 1-inch rule in sterile technique?
The area 1 inch around a sterile table edge is considered NOT sterile due to gravity. Do not place sterile items near table edges.
A sponge count is off by 1 after closure. What's the immediate action?
STOP closure immediately. Surgeon is notified. X-ray is ordered to rule out retained foreign body. Document the count discrepancy.
Patient in lithotomy position for 3 hours. What's the main complication risk?
Rhabdomyolysis and compartment syndrome from prolonged high leg elevation and thigh compression. Also DVT/thrombosis risk. Monitor postop for pain, swelling, rhabdo labs.
Signs of malignant hyperthermia during surgery include masseter rigidity and elevated CO₂. First intervention?
STOP anesthesia immediately. Hyperventilate with 100% O₂. Notify surgeon. Prepare dantrolene IV (2.5 mg/kg). Begin active cooling.
