Week 1 Content — Respiratory

Assessment & Priority Thinking

  • ★ ABCs always come first. Airway and breathing are assessed before comfort, pain, or any other concern. Post-epistaxis or post-trach decannulation: assess oxygen saturation and airway patency FIRST.
  • New-onset confusion in respiratory patients may indicate hypoxemia, not just agitation — reassess oxygenation before sedating.
  • Specimen quality over speed: Sputum culture needs a cough-produced specimen from the lower lungs, not saliva. C&S takes 48–72 hours, so getting it right the first time matters.

Tracheostomy Safety & Equipment

  • ★ Bedside safety for a new trach: obturator (for reinsertion guide), two spare tubes (one same size, one smaller for swelling), Ambu bag with trach adapter — not a face mask.
  • Accidental decannulation: insert obturator into OUTER cannula only, never leave it in place. Sequence: obturator in, extend neck, insert outer cannula, remove obturator immediately, assess lung sounds and O₂ sat.
  • Speaking valve requires deflated cuff so air can flow around it for phonation — inflated cuff blocks speech.

Procedures & Monitoring

  • ★ Bronchoscopy NPO is 8–12 hours (not 4 hours) because sedation requires full airway protection. Gag reflex must return before eating or drinking post-procedure.
  • ★ Suctioning duration: 10 seconds or less per pass to minimize airway trauma and hypoxemia. Pre-oxygenate before and between passes.
  • Prevent secretion thickening: adequate humidification (humid oxygen) and hydration (PO fluids or IV) work; frequent suctioning does NOT prevent thickening, only addresses it after it forms.

Temperature & Metabolic Demand

  • ★ Every degree of fever increases cellular oxygen demand. Normalizing temperature reduces the work the lungs have to do — critical for patients with limited oxygen reserves.
  • Hydration is dual-purpose: keeps secretions thin AND supports temperature regulation.

Comprehensive Equipment Assessment

  • Everything connected to the patient is part of your ongoing assessment: trach patency and cuff, Foley urine (color/volume/patency), NG tube drainage (character and function), IVs (patency/infiltration), chest tubes — do not assess one system in isolation.

Fundamentals — Big Concepts That Repeat

Assessment Skills & Procedures

  • ★ Specimen collection quality: Sputum = cough from lower lungs (not saliva). Blood cultures = two separate sites, sterile technique. Catheterized urine = straight cath or freshly voided from catheter port, NOT the drainage bag.
  • ★ Orthostatic vital signs: Lying (1 min), sitting (1 min), standing (3 min). Increase in HR >20 or drop in systolic >20 mmHg = orthostatic hypotension. Stop medication if severe.
  • Everything connected is assessed: Foley (urine color/volume/patency), NG tube (drainage character/function), IVs (patency/infiltration), chest tube (tidaling/output), trach (cuff/patency).

Metabolic & Oxygenation Concepts

  • ★ Fever increases oxygen demand. Every degree of temperature rise increases cellular metabolism and O₂ consumption — normalize fever in patients with limited oxygen reserves.
  • ★ Hydration is dual-purpose: keeps secretions thin (easier to cough/suction) AND supports temperature regulation and perfusion.
  • Immobility triggers a cascade: pressure injury risk, VTE risk, constipation, urinary retention, weakness. Address with frequent repositioning, DVT prophylaxis, early mobilization.
  • ★ Atypical illness in older adults: confusion instead of fever, confusion instead of chest pain with MI. Always investigate new-onset confusion as possible infection or acute illness.

Communication & Priority Thinking

  • ★ ABCs before everything else: airway and breathing always assessed first. ABCs before the neuro exam, ABCs before naloxone, ABCs before pain assessment.
  • SBAR when escalating to provider: Situation (what's happening now), Background (relevant history), Assessment (what you think), Recommendation (what you suggest).
  • The conversation IS the care: NURSE statements at end of life, weight-sensitive communication in obesity, discharge teaching for older adults — what you say is the nursing intervention.
