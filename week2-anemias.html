Anemias: Comprehensive Study Guide

Iron Deficiency, B12/Pernicious Anemia, Folate Deficiency, and Aplastic Anemia

📌 What IS Anemia?

CRITICAL CONCEPT: Anemia does NOT CAUSE low hematocrit. LOW HEMATOCRIT IS anemia. Don't confuse cause and effect.

Types:

  • Decreased RBC production
  • Increased RBC destruction (hemolysis)
  • Increased RBC loss (bleeding)

Acute vs. Chronic:

  • Chronic anemia: May be asymptomatic (body adapts)
  • Acute anemia: Many symptoms (body hasn't adapted)

🔴 IRON DEFICIENCY ANEMIA

Most Common Type of Anemia

Key Pathophysiology:

  • Iron deficiency → cells are small and pale (microcytic, hypochromic)
  • 70% of iron is in hemoglobin
  • 30% is stored as ferritin (iron reserves)

Causes:

  • Inadequate intake: Malnutrition, anorexia, financial constraints
  • Absorption issues: GI problems, surgery
  • Blood loss: Menstruation (women), GI bleeding, hemolysis

Clinical Manifestations:

  • #1 MOST COMMON: Pallor (paleness)
  • Look at conjunctiva for line of demarcation: Rich red = normal; Pale = anemia
  • Epithelial Atrophy:
    • Glossitis: Inflamed tongue (red, smooth, sore)
    • Angular cheilitis: Inflammation of lips, especially corners of mouth
    • Brittle hair and nails
Unique to Iron Deficiency:
  • Pica: Craving for non-food items (dirt, ice, starch)
  • Pagophagia: Specific craving for ice - classic sign on exams

Lab Findings:

  • Microcytic, hypochromic cells
  • Low serum iron
  • Low ferritin = MOST SENSITIVE TEST
  • Low transferrin

Nursing Assessment:

  • Trend lab values (is H&H going up or down?)
  • Signs of pallor (check conjunctiva)
  • Epithelial atrophy signs (tongue, lips, hair, nails)
  • Ask about unusual cravings (pica, pagophagia)

Nursing Interventions:

  • Increase iron-rich foods (red meat, liver, fortified cereals, legumes, spinach)
  • Oral iron supplementation (preferred - inexpensive, easier)
  • Teach about medication side effects

Patient Education - CRITICAL FOR EXAM:

  • Iron-rich foods: Red meat, liver, fortified cereals, legumes, spinach
  • Vitamin C enhances absorption: Take with orange juice for better absorption
  • Expected side effect: Stool turns BLACK (not melena - this is expected!)
  • Teach difference: Melena (blood - malodorous, sticky) vs. iron-blackened stool (expected, not harmful)
  • GI side effects: Constipation most common; nausea with empty stomach
  • Timing: Take with food if nauseous BUT preferably on empty stomach for better absorption

💜 B12 DEFICIENCY / PERNICIOUS ANEMIA

CRITICAL: Pernicious anemia is autoimmune and LIFELONG. It requires lifelong B12 replacement.

Key Pathophysiology:

  • Intrinsic Factor: Produced by parietal cells in stomach; REQUIRED for B12 absorption
  • Pernicious Anemia: Autoimmune disease; antibodies destroy intrinsic factor
  • Cell morphology: Megaloblastic (large but fragile membrane)
  • Result: LIFELONG condition requiring ongoing treatment

Causes of B12 Deficiency:

  • Pernicious anemia (autoimmune) - #1 most common
  • Lack of intrinsic factor
  • Malabsorption (Crohn's disease, bowel surgery)
  • Dietary (strict vegans - B12 only in animal products)
  • Chronic alcohol abuse (poor nutritional intake)
  • Gastrectomy (loss of parietal cells)

Clinical Manifestations:

GI Manifestations:

  • Nausea, vomiting, anorexia
  • Abdominal pain
  • Glossitis, cheilitis
⚠️ UNIQUE TO B12 - NEUROLOGIC MANIFESTATIONS (NOT seen with iron deficiency or folate):
  • Paresthesia: Numbness and tingling in fingers and toes
  • Ataxia: Uncoordinated movements, balance problems ("looks drunk")
  • CRITICAL: These neurologic symptoms can be IRREVERSIBLE if not treated

Lab Findings:

  • Megaloblastic anemia (large cells but normalchromic)
  • Low serum B12
  • Positive antibodies to intrinsic factor (confirms pernicious anemia)

Nursing Assessment:

  • NEUROLOGIC EXAM IS CRITICAL: Paresthesia? Ataxia? Balance problems?
  • Assess gait and coordination
  • Assess for GI symptoms

Nursing Interventions:

  • B12 replacement:
    • Oral or IM route
    • IM route preferred if severe neurologic problems
    • Megadoses: ~1,000 mcg (but only 2.4 mcg absorbed)
    • Evidence shows oral and IM similar effectiveness for maintenance
  • Lifelong treatment for pernicious anemia

Patient Education:

  • Vegan patients: B12 only found in animal products; need supplementation
  • Pernicious anemia is lifelong: Requires ongoing treatment; won't go away
  • Report neurologic symptoms EARLY: Paresthesia, ataxia, balance problems
  • Importance of compliance: Missing doses can lead to irreversible nerve damage

🟢 FOLATE DEFICIENCY

Key Pathophysiology:

  • Usually due to decreased INTAKE (not absorption problem)
  • Cell morphology: Macrocytic, normalchromic (same appearance as B12 but WITHOUT neurologic symptoms)

Causes:

  • Decreased intake: Pregnant women (increased needs), alcohol abuse
  • Malabsorption: Rare

Clinical Manifestations:

  • Very insidious onset
  • Similar to B12 BUT WITHOUT neurologic problems
  • GI symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, abdominal pain
  • Glossitis, cheilitis

Lab Findings:

  • Macrocytic, normalchromic
  • Low serum folate
KEY DIFFERENTIATOR FOR EXAM: Check for neurologic problems to differentiate folate from B12:
  • Paresthesia, ataxia, balance problems = B12
  • No neurologic symptoms = Folate

Nursing Interventions:

  • Folic acid supplementation (oral)
  • Increase dietary intake

Patient Education:

  • Folate-rich foods: Green leafy vegetables, legumes, grains, orange juice, nuts
  • Pregnant women: Extra folate needed; prevents neural tube defects

⚫ APLASTIC ANEMIA

Much more serious than other anemias. Requires aggressive management.

Key Pathophysiology:

  • Problem with precursors to blood cells in bone marrow (not the cells themselves)
  • Results in PANCYTOPENIA: Decreased RBCs, WBCs, AND platelets simultaneously
  • UNIQUE TO APLASTIC ANEMIA: The only anemia with all three cell lines down
  • Idiopathic (cause unknown) in most cases; sometimes autoimmune

Clinical Manifestations:

  • Anemia symptoms: Fatigue, weakness, pallor
  • Leukopenia/Neutropenia risk: AT RISK FOR INFECTION (due to low WBC)
  • Thrombocytopenia risk: AT RISK FOR BLEEDING (due to low platelets)
  • UNIQUE COMBINATION: Only anemia presenting with both bleeding AND infection risks

Lab Findings:

  • Normalcytic, normalchromic cells
  • BUT very FEW of all three cell lines
  • Very low WBCs, very low platelets, very low RBCs
  • Requires bone marrow biopsy for diagnosis

Nursing Assessment:

  • Signs of infection: Fever, elevated WBC differential
  • Signs of bleeding: Petechiae, ecchymosis, gum bleeding, nose bleeds
  • Fatigue and activity tolerance
  • Signs of anemia (pallor, dyspnea)

Nursing Diagnoses (For NCLEX):

  • Risk for infection (neutropenia)
  • Risk for bleeding (thrombocytopenia)
  • Activity intolerance (anemia)
  • Anxiety (life-threatening condition)

Nursing Interventions & Collaborative Care:

  • Bone marrow biopsy: Definitive diagnosis
  • Platelet transfusions if bleeding risk
  • RBC transfusions for severe anemia
  • Possible stem cell transplant for cure
  • Monitor closely for infection and bleeding
  • Protective precautions: Neutropenic precautions if severe

Complications:

  • Life-threatening bleeding
  • Life-threatening infection
  • Ongoing supportive care needed
