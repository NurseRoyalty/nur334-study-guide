Week 6 covers the GI tract from top to bottom: how we test it, what goes wrong in the upper tract (reflux, hernia, ulcers, bleeding), and what goes wrong in the lower tract (IBD, obstruction, cancer, ostomies, diverticular disease). Each section links to its full topic page.

1. GI Labs, Diagnostics & Bariatric Surgery

  • Upper GI / small bowel follow-through, barium swallow, barium enema: NPO before; after — push fluids (barium constipates) and teach white stool. Use Gastrografin, not barium, if perforation is suspected. Barium enema needs the bowel cleared (enemas until clear, clear liquids)
  • Modified barium swallow: tests swallowing with different consistencies (e.g., dysphagia after stroke)
  • Gastric emptying study: egg with trace radioactivity; detects gastroparesis; patient lies flat
  • EGD: NPO 8 h + consent before; NPO until the gag reflex returns (2–4 h) after; watch for bleeding and perforation. Can cauterize bleeds and varices
  • Colonoscopy: thorough bowel prep; gas afterward; watch for perforation/bleeding
  • Stool: occult blood (guaiac), culture (C. diff), ova and parasites
  • Bariatric criteria: BMI ≥ 40, or ≥ 35 with a serious obesity-related condition. Roux-en-Y = gold standard, restrictive + malabsorptive, 15 mL pouch, lifelong vitamins, risk of dumping syndrome. Post-op: mobility, infection/dehiscence watch, NG to suction, slow diet progression, DASH diet long-term

2. GERD, Hiatal Hernia & Peptic Ulcer Disease

Drug classExamplesPoint to remember

CytoprotectiveSucralfateCoats the stomach
ProkineticMetoclopramideSpeeds gastric emptying
  • GERD: loose LES → acid refluxes. Heartburn = most common symptom. Complications: esophagitis, Barrett's esophagus (30–40× esophageal cancer risk; biopsy), cough/bronchospasm. Often diagnosed with a 2-week PPI trial
  • GERD teaching: quit smoking, small meals, no fluids with meals, avoid fatty foods and late snacks, upright 2–3 h after meals, HOB on 4–6 inch blocks, weight loss
  • Refractory GERD: LINX magnetic ring; Nissen fundoplication
  • Hiatal hernia: stomach through the diaphragm; increased abdominal pressure; treated like GERD
  • PUD: HCl + pepsin erode the mucosa. H. pylori and NSAIDs are the main causes. 80% duodenal. Dx: EGD, urea breath/stool test, biopsy = gold standard
  • HOP: Hemorrhage (most common; pain may briefly ease), Obstruction (near pylorus; NG to LWS), Perforation (sudden severe pain, board-like abdomen, rebound tenderness, peritonitis in 6–12 h)
  • Dumping syndrome: after gastric surgery; hyperosmolar food → fluid shift → diarrhea, dizziness, palpitations; hypoglycemia ~2 h later. Teach 6 small meals, no fluids with meals, avoid simple sugars, more protein and fat, rest after eating

3. Upper GI Bleed, Crohn's, UC & Intestinal Obstruction

Upper GI bleed

  • Blood clues: bright red hematemesis = active; coffee-ground = older/stopped; melena = black tarry (digested up to ~8 h); occult = guaiac
  • Causes: ulcers (~50%), esophageal varices (Blakemore tube), H. pylori, NSAIDs/aspirin/steroids, stress-related mucosal disease (ICU → PPI)
  • Nursing: VS trends and orthostatics; shock signs (tachycardia, thirst, cool clammy skin, restlessness); H&H and BUN (rises with GI bleed); 18-gauge IV; prepare for endoscopy — hemostasis is first-line
Crohn'sUlcerative colitis
WhereMouth to anus; terminal ileumColon only; starts in the rectum
PatternSkip lesions, cobblestoneContinuous
DepthTransmural → fistulas, strictures, perforationMucosal → bleeding, toxic megacolon
SurgeryResection; not curativeColectomy is curative
  • Flare: rest the bowel (NPO + IV fluids), control inflammation, treat infection, correct malnutrition. Then high-calorie, high-protein, low-residue diet
  • TPN: only if the gut can't be used; central line; dedicated line; glucose checks about every 6 h; start and stop gradually
  • Drug of choice: sulfasalazine; also steroids, antimicrobials, immunosuppressants, immunomodulators
  • Obstruction: mechanical (adhesions = #1) or paralytic ileus. Decompress with NG to low wall suction, fix fluids/electrolytes, remove obstruction (resection with end-to-end anastomosis) if needed

4. Colorectal Cancer & Bowel Ostomies

  • Risk factors: red/processed meat, low fruits/vegetables, obesity, inactivity, alcohol, smoking, IBD, genetics
  • Signs: insidious — anemia, bleeding, pain, change in bowel habits; may present as obstruction or perforation
  • Screening: colonoscopy (gold standard) at 45, every 10 years; yearly stool test if colonoscopy is refused
  • Surgery: AP resection → permanent colostomy; low anterior resection → sphincter preserved
  • Ostomies: location determines output — ileostomy (RLQ) liquid and caustic; sigmoid/descending colostomy (LLQ) formed. End stoma (± Hartmann's pouch), loop stoma (rod; proximal = stool, distal = mucus), double-barrel (two separate stomas)
  • Stoma care: assess color, swelling, bleeding; skin barrier; wafer cut to exact stoma size; involve the ostomy nurse; expect a slow emotional adjustment

5. Diverticulosis & Diverticulitis

  • Diverticulosis: out-pouchings; often asymptomatic. High fiber, activity, avoid constipation; nuts and seeds are OK
  • Diverticulitis: a flare — LLQ pain, possible mass, infection signs (older adults may only be confused)
  • Treatment: NPO, IV fluids and antibiotics; watch for abscess, bleeding, peritonitis (rupture); surgery with possible temporary ostomy

Week 6 red flags: a rigid, board-like abdomen (perforation/peritonitis) · rising heart rate, falling H&H, rising BUN (GI bleed) · drinking before the gag reflex returns after EGD · a restless patient · no stool or gas for more than 24 hours after bowel surgery.

Self-Test Flashcards

Click a card to flip it and check your answer.

Which contrast is used for an upper GI when perforation is suspected?
Gastrografin — barium is avoided so it doesn't end up in the peritoneum.
What teaching follows any barium study?
Drink lots of fluids (barium is constipating) and expect white stool.
After an EGD, when can the client eat or drink?
Not until the gag reflex returns (about 2–4 hours).
What are the bariatric surgery BMI criteria?
BMI ≥40, or ≥35 with at least one severe obesity-related condition.
Why is the Roux-en-Y the 'gold standard'?
It's the most common and both restrictive (15 mL pouch) and malabsorptive, with 60–70% weight loss in 1–2 years.
What does metoclopramide do?
Speeds gastric emptying (prokinetic).
Name the upper GI drug classes and an example of each.
Antacids; H2 blockers (famotidine); PPIs (pantoprazole); cytoprotective (sucralfate); prokinetic (metoclopramide).
What is the most common symptom of GERD?
Heartburn.
Why is Barrett's esophagus significant?
Metaplasia from chronic reflux; 30–40× higher risk of esophageal cancer.
What are the main causes of peptic ulcer disease?
H. pylori and NSAIDs (worse together); also alcohol, smoking, family history.
What are the complications of PUD?
HOP — Hemorrhage, Obstruction, Perforation.
What does a rigid, board-like abdomen suggest?
Perforation with peritonitis — an emergency.
How do you prevent dumping syndrome?
6 small meals, no fluids with meals, avoid simple sugars, more protein and fat, rest after eating.
Coffee-ground emesis vs bright red hematemesis?
Coffee-ground = older bleeding or bleeding that has stopped; bright red = recent or ongoing.
Why can BUN rise with a GI bleed?
Digested blood in the GI tract raises BUN.
What is first-line management of an upper GI bleed?
Endoscopic hemostasis (e.g., cautery).
Crohn's vs UC: location and pattern?
Crohn's: mouth to anus, skip lesions, transmural. UC: colon only, starts in the rectum, continuous.
When is TPN used instead of tube feeding?
Only when the gut can't be used. If the gut works, use it.
What is the #1 cause of intestinal obstruction?
Surgical adhesions.
When should an average-risk client start colonoscopy screening?
At 45, every 10 years.
A client has a right lower quadrant stoma. What output is expected?
Liquid, near-continuous, caustic output — an ileostomy.
Which diet helps diverticulosis?
High fiber (nuts and seeds don't need to be avoided).
