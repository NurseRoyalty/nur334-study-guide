Section 1: High-Level Overview

A comprehensive summary of highly testable material across all Week 5 topics.

HIV/AIDS Overview

  • CD4 Count Thresholds: Normal ≥ 500, Early symptoms 200-500, AIDS-defining < 200
  • AIDS-Defining Conditions: PCP, toxoplasmosis, CMV, Cryptococcal meningitis, tuberculosis (TB), oral candidiasis
  • Transmission: Blood, sexual contact, mother-to-child; NOT by casual contact
  • Treatment Goal: Suppress viral load to undetectable (< 50 copies/mL) with ART (Antiretroviral Therapy)

Skin Conditions Overview

  • Melanoma (Highly Testable): ABCDE rule — Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter > 6mm, Evolving
  • Bacterial Infections: Impetigo (highly contagious), cellulitis (spreading edema), erysipelas (distinct borders)
  • Fungal Infections: Candida (HIV-related), tinea (dermatophyte)
  • Viral: HSV, varicella-zoster virus (VZV), molluscum contagiosum
  • Parasitic: Scabies (mites), lice

Wound Healing & Burns Overview

  • Wound Healing Phases: Hemostasis/Inflammatory (0-4 days), Proliferative (4-21 days), Maturation/Remodeling (21 days-2 years)
  • Infection vs. Normal Healing: Infection = fever, purulent drainage, increasing pain; Normal = minimal drainage, decreasing pain
  • Burn Depth: Superficial (red, painful), Partial-thickness (blistering, painful), Full-thickness (painless, eschar formation)
  • Rule of 9s & Parkland Formula: Quick assessment and fluid replacement calculation

Section 2: HIV Infection

Pathophysiology & CD4 Staging

  • HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) attacks CD4+ T cells, which coordinate immune response
  • CD4 Count: Normal 500-1500 cells/μL → Progresses to < 200 (AIDS-defining)
  • Acute Retroviral Syndrome: Occurs 2-4 weeks after infection — fever, fatigue, rash, lymphadenopathy
  • Clinical Latency: Can last 10+ years with no symptoms but ongoing viral replication

Opportunistic Infections by CD4 Count

CD4 CountPrimary OI Risk
> 500TB, acute bacterial infections
200-500Oral candidiasis, cryptococcal disease
< 200PCP (Pneumocystis pneumonia), toxoplasmosis
< 100CMV (cytomegalovirus), MAC (Mycobacterium avium)

Common Skin Manifestations in HIV

  • Kaposi Sarcoma (KS): Purple/brown lesions, HHV-8 associated, highly associated with advanced AIDS (CD4 < 50)
  • Oral Candidiasis (Thrush): White patches in mouth; occurs when CD4 < 200; risk factor for esophageal candidiasis
  • Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV): Recurrent or severe lesions; distinguish from VZV (dermatomal distribution)
  • Molluscum Contagiosum: Umbilicated lesions more common and severe in advanced AIDS

Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)

  • Goal: Suppress viral load to undetectable (< 50 copies/mL) within 6 months
  • Adherence is Critical: Missing doses → resistance → treatment failure
  • IRIS (Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome): Occurs when CD4 rebounds rapidly; can paradoxically worsen existing OIs
  • Lactic Acidosis: Rare but serious side effect from nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs)

Section 3: Skin Conditions

Melanoma & the ABCDE Rule

  • A - Asymmetry: One half doesn't match the other
  • B - Border Irregularity: Ragged, notched, or blurred edges
  • C - Color Variation: Multiple colors (brown, black, red, white, blue)
  • D - Diameter: > 6 mm (pencil eraser size)
  • E - Evolving: Changing in size, shape, or color

Bacterial Skin Infections

InfectionCharacteristicsNursing Considerations
ImpetigoHoney-crusted, highly contagious, superficialIsolate; contact precautions; topical/oral abx
CellulitisSpreading erythema, edema, warmth, pain; no defined borderSystemic antibiotics; elevate affected area; monitor spread
ErysipelasRaised, well-demarcated border; bright red; often facialBeta-hemolytic Streptococcus; systemic antibiotics

Fungal, Viral, and Parasitic Infections

Fungal

  • Candida: White patches (mouth), erythema (skin folds); thrush risk with CD4 < 200
  • Tinea: Ring-shaped; scaly; KOH prep confirms; treat topical/systemic azoles

Viral & Parasitic

  • HSV: Painful vesicles, recurrent; can be severe in immunocompromised
  • Scabies: Mites; intense itching, burrow lines; treat household/contacts

Section 4: Wound Management & Burns

Wound Healing Phases

PhaseTimelineKey Events
Hemostasis/Inflammatory0-4 daysClotting, inflammation, WBC infiltration
Proliferative4-21 daysCollagen deposition, angiogenesis, granulation tissue
Maturation/Remodeling21 days-2 yearsCollagen cross-linking, wound strengthening, scar formation

Infection vs. Normal Healing

Signs of Infection

  • Fever, increased pain
  • Purulent or foul-smelling drainage
  • Erythema, warmth, swelling beyond wound borders
  • Delayed healing
  • Culture = significant growth

Normal Healing

  • Minimal serous drainage initially
  • Decreasing pain over time
  • Granulation tissue (red, bumpy)
  • Edges approximating/closing
  • Culture = normal flora only

Burn Classification

DepthLayerAppearancePain
SuperficialEpidermis onlyRed, dryVery painful
Partial-ThicknessEpidermis + DermisBlistered, moist, pale pinkVery painful
Full-ThicknessThrough all layersCharred, leathery, escharPainless (nerve destruction)

Burn Management: Rule of 9s & Parkland Formula

  • Rule of 9s: Rapidly estimate % BSA burned (Head/Neck 9%, Trunk 36%, Each arm 9%, Each leg 18%, Genitalia 1%)
  • Parkland Formula: 4 mL × kg body weight × % BSA burned = mL LR IV fluids in first 24 hours (give half in first 8 hours)
  • Goal: Urine output 0.5 mL/kg/hr (adults) or 1 mL/kg/hr (children) to assess fluid adequacy
  • Inhalation Injury: Monitor for respiratory compromise; carbon monoxide poisoning; thermal injury to airways

Section 5: Big Picture Overview

Highly Testable Concepts for NCLEX-NGN

  • HIV CD4 Count Interpretation: Know OI prophylaxis/treatment thresholds; understand IRIS risk
  • Melanoma ABCDE Rule: Most commonly tested skin cancer concept; always look for evolving lesions
  • Cellulitis vs. Erysipelas: Distinguish borders and presentation; treatment implications
  • Wound Infection Signs: Fever + purulent drainage is classic; don't confuse inflammation with infection
  • Burn Severity & Parkland Formula: Essential for fluid management; calculate accurately
  • ART Adherence: HIV treatment success depends on compliance; resistance develops with missed doses
HIV CD4 Risk Levels (Mnemonic: "POCKF"):
  • PCP (Pneumocystis) - CD4 < 200
  • Oral
  • CMV - CD4 < 50
  • Kaposi
  • Fungal
Clinical Red Flags: Always assess for inhalation injury in burn patients (stridor, singed nasal hairs, carbonaceous sputum). Monitor urine output closely with Parkland formula — inadequate urine output = inadequate fluid replacement = acute tubular necrosis risk.
