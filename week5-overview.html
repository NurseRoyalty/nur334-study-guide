Week 5 Content Module

Week 5 · Overview

Learning Outcomes: By the end of this week, you will be able to assess and manage patients with HIV/AIDS, identify and treat common skin conditions, manage acute and chronic wounds, and provide emergency care for burn injuries.

Week 5 Topics at a Glance

This week focuses on four critical areas of nursing practice that require immediate assessment and intervention skills. These conditions frequently present in clinical settings and are prevalent on the NCLEX-NGN examination.

Integrated Concepts for Week 5

Safety & Infection Prevention

All four topics require strict adherence to standard precautions, appropriate use of PPE, and understanding transmission routes. HIV requires additional bloodborne pathogen precautions; skin conditions require contact precautions; wounds require aseptic technique; burns require protection from infection.

Assessment & Prioritization

Rapid assessment is critical. For HIV patients: CD4+ count interpretation is paramount. For skin: distinguish infectious from noninfectious. For wounds: assess depth, drainage, edges. For burns: calculate body surface area (BSA) and anticipate complications.

Fluid & Electrolyte Management

Major theme across all topics. HIV/AIDS patients experience fluid shifts from diarrhea. Skin conditions affect fluid loss. Wounds require moisture balance. Burns demand immediate fluid resuscitation using precise formulas.

Clinical Reasoning Framework

Use this framework throughout Week 5:

  1. Recognize: What condition is the patient presenting with?
  2. Interpret: What diagnostic findings support this? What are the risk factors?
  3. Respond: What are the immediate nursing priorities?
  4. Reflect: What are potential complications? How will I monitor?

Self-Test Flashcards

Question
CD4+ is 45 cells/uL. What is the most dangerous OI and why?
Answer
CMV - disseminated disease is life-threatening at this level. Requires IV therapy immediately.
Question
Stage 3 vs Stage 4 pressure injury - what's the key difference for treatment?
Answer
Stage 3: dermis exposed (can heal with wound care). Stage 4: muscle/bone exposed (requires surgical consult and likely grafting).
Question
80 kg patient, 40% BSA burn. First 8-hour fluid rate?
Answer
Parkland: 4 × 40 × 80 = 12,800 mL total; first 8 hours = 6,400 mL (titrate based on UOP, not time).
Question
Patient with HIV has dysphagia and white oral patches. What prophylaxis?
Answer
Candida esophagitis - start fluconazole. Also indicates need for CD4 testing and OI prophylaxis assessment.
Question
Patient with red, bumpy granulation tissue after burn. What dressing?
Answer
Moist non-adherent (foam, hydrocolloid, alginate) - promotes healing. Never dry gauze (damages tissue).
Question
Cellulitis vs erysipelas - which has well-demarcated borders?
Answer
Erysipelas - well-demarcated, raised, superficial. Cellulitis is deeper with poorly demarcated edges.
Question
Dermatomal vesicular rash. What precautions?
Answer
Herpes zoster (shingles) - Contact + Airborne precautions until lesions crust over (7-10 days).
Question
Wound with undermining/tunneling. What does this indicate?
Answer
Non-healing wound - likely infection or ongoing pressure. Requires probe assessment to determine true dimensions.
Question
Post-burn day 2: UOP 0.3 mL/kg/hr. What's the problem and action?
Answer
Inadequate resuscitation - risk of AKI. Increase IV rate immediately to achieve goal UOP (0.5 mL/kg/hr for deep burns).
