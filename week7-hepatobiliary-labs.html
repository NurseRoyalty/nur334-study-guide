ERCP (Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography)

An endoscope goes through the mouth and stomach to the duodenum, then backward (retrograde) up into the biliary tree. It visualizes and accesses the pancreatic, hepatic, and common bile ducts and can find or treat stones.

Before

  • NPO
  • Signed consent (invasive)
  • Sedation; the throat is numbed

After

  • Frequent vital signs — watch for perforation and, later, infection
  • Pancreatitis is the most common complication (the procedure irritates the pancreas)
  • NPO until the gag reflex returns, about 2–4 hours

Liver Biopsy

The liver is very vascular, and patients with liver disease often can't make prothrombin — so bleeding is the main risk.

Before / during

  • Check coagulation studies (PT/INR)
  • Type and crossmatch
  • Signed consent; baseline vital signs
  • During needle insertion: hold the breath after expiration; the arm is raised over the head

After

  • Frequent vital signs — a rising heart rate is the first sign of internal bleeding (BP falls later)
  • Position on the right side (pressure on the liver)
  • Keep flat for 12–24 hours
  • Complications: bleeding, pneumothorax (the liver sits next to the lung), peritonitis, shock

Self-Test Flashcards

Click a card to flip it and check your answer.

Which test is first-line for suspected gallstones, and how accurate is it?
Abdominal ultrasound — about 95% accurate. The client is NPO for 8 hours beforehand.
A gallbladder ultrasound is negative but gallstones are still suspected. Which test comes next?
A HIDA scan (hepatobiliary scintigraphy), which shows whether the common bile duct is patent.
What should the nurse check and teach before a CT with contrast?
Check BUN and creatinine (contrast is nephrotoxic); warn about the warm flush; encourage fluids afterward; watch for a reaction.
Who cannot have an MRI?
Clients with metal implants or who are pregnant.
What is the most common complication after an ERCP?
Pancreatitis.
After an ERCP, when can the client eat or drink?
When the gag reflex returns, usually in 2–4 hours.
Which labs must be reviewed before a liver biopsy?
Coagulation studies (PT/INR); the client should also be typed and crossmatched.
How is a client positioned after a liver biopsy?
On the right side, kept flat for 12–24 hours, to put pressure on the biopsy site.
After a liver biopsy, the client's heart rate climbs and breath sounds are diminished on the right. What should the nurse suspect?
Internal bleeding and/or a pneumothorax. Notify the provider.
Which liver enzyme is most specific to the liver?
ALT. (AST and alkaline phosphatase are less specific.)
How do conjugated and unconjugated bilirubin differ?
Conjugated (direct) has been processed by the liver, is water-soluble, appears in urine, and rises with obstruction. Unconjugated (indirect) is not water-soluble and rises with problems in or before the liver.
How do amylase and lipase behave in acute pancreatitis?
Amylase rises within 12 hours, peaks at 24, and normalizes in 48–72 hours. Lipase rises more slowly and stays elevated longer.
