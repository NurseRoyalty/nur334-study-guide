Transfusion Reactions: Comprehensive Study Guide

Acute, Delayed, and Nursing Management

📌 What is a Transfusion Reaction?

Transfusion reaction = Immune or non-immune response to blood products

Can occur within minutes (acute) or hours to days later (delayed). Some reactions are life-threatening emergencies.

The Core Risk: Patient's immune system recognizes transfused blood as foreign and reacts = fever, chills, hemolysis, shock, DIC in severe cases

🔴 Types of Transfusion Reactions

ACUTE Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (AHTR)

MOST SERIOUS acute reaction - occurs within MINUTES to hours - MEDICAL EMERGENCY

Cause: ABO incompatibility (wrong blood type transfused) OR prior sensitization to donor RBCs

Signs & Symptoms (EARLY - Watch for these!):

  • During or immediately after transfusion: Fever, chills, chest pain, back/flank pain (CLASSIC for hemolysis)
  • Shock symptoms: Tachycardia, hypotension, pallor, diaphoresis
  • Hemoglobinuria: Dark/cola-colored urine (hemoglobin in urine from RBC lysis)
  • Dyspnea, wheezing: Respiratory distress

Laboratory Findings:

  • Positive direct Coombs test (antibodies attached to RBCs)
  • Elevated indirect bilirubin (from hemolysis)
  • Decreased hematocrit (RBCs destroyed)
  • Elevated LDH (from cell breakdown)
  • LOW haptoglobin (depleted by binding free hemoglobin)

IMMEDIATE NURSING ACTIONS - STOP:

S = Stop transfusion IMMEDIATELY
T = Take vital signs and stay with patient
O = Obtain blood & urine samples for lab verification
P = Provider notification - STAT; IV access, fluids, monitor urine output

Ongoing Care:

  • Aggressive IV hydration: Maintain urine output ≥200mL/hr to prevent acute kidney injury
  • Diuretics: Furosemide may be ordered to increase urine flow
  • Urinary alkalinization: Sodium bicarbonate to prevent hemoglobin precipitation in kidneys
  • Monitor for DIC: Coagulation studies (PT, PTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer)
  • Monitor for AKI: BUN, creatinine, urine output closely
  • Supportive care: Oxygen, vasopressors if hypotensive

Febrile Non-Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (FNHTR)

MOST COMMON acute transfusion reaction (1-2% of all transfusions)

Cause: Reaction to WBC antigens or cytokines in donor blood - NOT actual hemolysis

Signs & Symptoms:

  • Fever: Usually modest increase (1-2°C above baseline)
  • Chills, rigor
  • Headache, myalgia (muscle aches)
  • NO hemoglobinuria, NO back pain (key differentiator from AHTR)

Laboratory:

  • Negative direct Coombs test (key difference from AHTR)
  • Normal hematocrit, LDH, bilirubin (no actual hemolysis)

Nursing Actions:

  • Stop transfusion
  • Verify patient identity and blood type
  • Acetaminophen or ibuprofen for fever
  • Usually can RESUME transfusion after fever response if FNHTR suspected (not AHTR)
  • Premedicate future transfusions with antipyretics
  • Leukoreduced blood products for future transfusions (removes WBCs)

Allergic/Anaphylactic Reactions

Mild allergic common; anaphylaxis RARE but LIFE-THREATENING

Cause: Immune response to foreign proteins in donor plasma (IgE antibodies)

MILD Allergic Reaction:

  • Urticaria (hives), pruritus (itching)
  • Flushing
  • Mild respiratory symptoms
  • Nursing: Stop transfusion, antihistamine (diphenhydramine), resume when symptoms resolve

ANAPHYLACTIC Reaction (True Emergency):

  • Airway compromise: Stridor, laryngeal edema, difficulty swallowing
  • Severe hypotension: Shock state
  • Severe bronchospasm: Wheezing, respiratory distress
  • Angioedema: Swelling of lips, tongue, face
  • Nursing: STOP immediately, call 911/provider STAT, IM epinephrine 0.3-0.5mg (IM preferred), IV access, fluids, monitor closely
  • Future transfusions: Use washed RBCs (plasma removed) to prevent recurrence

Acute Lung Injury (Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury - TRALI)

RARE but SERIOUS - can be life-threatening respiratory emergency

Cause: Immune-mediated reaction causing pulmonary edema; occurs within 6 hours of transfusion

Signs & Symptoms:

  • Acute dyspnea, tachypnea, hypoxia
  • Fever, chills
  • Pulmonary edema: Pink frothy sputum, crackles
  • Hypotension
  • No cardiac cause for pulmonary edema (key differentiator from CHF)

Nursing Actions:

  • Stop transfusion immediately
  • High-flow oxygen, may need intubation
  • Diuretics typically NOT helpful (non-cardiogenic edema)
  • Supportive respiratory care
  • Monitor for recovery (usually 48-96 hours)

Bacterial Contamination/Septic Shock

RARE but EXTREMELY SERIOUS - donor blood contaminated with bacteria

Cause: Bacteria in blood product (from donor or collection contamination)

Signs & Symptoms:

  • Sudden high fever (104-106°F+), rigors
  • Severe hypotension, shock
  • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain
  • Headache, altered mental status
  • Occurs within 30 min to few hours

Nursing Actions (SEPTIC SHOCK PROTOCOL):

  • STOP transfusion immediately
  • Blood cultures from patient and blood bag
  • Broad-spectrum antibiotics STAT
  • Aggressive fluid resuscitation and vasopressors
  • ICU admission for monitoring

DELAYED Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction (DHTR)

Occurs DAYS to WEEKS after transfusion

Cause: Immune memory response - patient previously sensitized but antibodies no longer detectable at time of transfusion

Signs & Symptoms (Subtle):

  • Unexplained fever, jaundice, dark urine
  • Decreased hematocrit (lack of expected transfusion benefit)
  • Usually mild compared to AHTR

Nursing Actions:

  • Recognize pattern: transfusion given, then days later mild symptoms + unexpected low hct
  • Lab confirmation: positive Coombs, elevated indirect bilirubin
  • Usually self-limited; supportive care
  • For future transfusions: cross-match against patient's antibody panel

⏱️ Transfusion Safety: Nursing Monitoring Protocol

BEFORE Transfusion:

  • Verify patient ID with two identifiers
  • Check blood type compatibility
  • Inspect blood product: clarity, color, leakage, expiration
  • Baseline vital signs
  • Establish IV access (18-20 gauge for PRBCs)

DURING Transfusion:

  • Start slowly: First 50mL over 10 minutes (highest risk period)
  • Stay with patient: First 15 minutes critical for detecting reaction
  • Monitor vitals: Every 15 minutes during transfusion
  • Observe for symptoms: Fever, chills, dyspnea, chest pain, back pain, hemoglobinuria
  • Rate: Usually 1-2 hours per unit (max 4 hours - blood expires in refrigerator after 4h at room temp)

AFTER Transfusion:

  • Post-transfusion vital signs
  • Monitor for delayed reactions for 24-48 hours
  • Assess for transfusion benefit (hematocrit increase)
  • Document: product, time, patient tolerance

📝 Key Takeaways

  • ✓ AHTR vs FNHTR: Back pain + hemoglobinuria = AHTR (life threat); fever only = FNHTR (resume after meds)
  • ✓ First sign of AHTR = back/flank pain (from hemolysis) - NOT just fever
  • ✓ STOP-Verify-Labs protocol: Stop transfusion, verify compatibility, get labs (Coombs, bilirubin)
  • ✓ Anaphylaxis = IM epinephrine STAT + washed RBCs for future
  • ✓ TRALI = pulmonary edema within 6 hours, diuretics NOT helpful
  • ✓ Bacterial contamination = septic shock appearance
  • ✓ DHTR = occurs days later, unexplained fever + low hct
  • ✓ Transfusion monitoring: Stay with patient first 15 min, slow start (50mL over 10 min), vitals every 15 min
  • ✓ Cola-colored urine = hemoglobinuria (sign of hemolysis)
  • ✓ Positive direct Coombs = hemolytic reaction (AHTR or DHTR)
