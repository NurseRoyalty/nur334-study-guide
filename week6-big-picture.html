The Big Picture: One GI Tract, Top to Bottom

Every Week 6 topic sits somewhere along one tube. If you know where a problem is, you can usually predict what the patient looks like, what comes out, and what the nurse does.

Connecting the Dots

  • GERD → Barrett's esophagus → esophageal cancer: untreated reflux changes the cells
  • H. pylori / NSAIDs → PUD → hemorrhage → upper GI bleed: ulcers cause about half of upper GI bleeds
  • Gastric surgery (PUD surgery, Roux-en-Y) → dumping syndrome
  • IBD → colorectal cancer risk; UC → colectomy → ileostomy or J-pouch
  • Crohn's strictures, surgical adhesions, tumors → intestinal obstruction
  • Colorectal cancer / diverticulitis / obstruction → bowel resection → possible ostomy
  • Liver disease → portal hypertension → esophageal varices → life-threatening upper GI bleed

NCLEX-NGN Clinical Judgment Scenarios

Scenario 1: "The pain is better"

  • Cues: Client with PUD; HR 76 → 88 → 102 over the shift; says the ulcer pain is "finally better"; NG drainage turning red
  • Analysis: Hemorrhage — pain can ease briefly because blood neutralizes acid
  • Actions: Notify provider; NG to low wall suction; 18-gauge IV; check H&H and BUN; prepare for endoscopy

Scenario 2: Board-like abdomen

  • Cues: Sudden severe epigastric pain radiating to the back, rigid board-like abdomen, shallow rapid breathing, weak rapid pulse, absent bowel sounds
  • Analysis: Perforated ulcer → peritonitis (within 6–12 hours)
  • Actions: Emergency — notify the provider immediately, keep NPO, monitor vital signs closely

Scenario 3: After dessert

  • Cues: 30 minutes after cake, a client with a Roux-en-Y has diarrhea, dizziness, sweating, palpitations; 2 hours later is shaky
  • Analysis: Dumping syndrome, then postprandial hypoglycemia
  • Actions: Teach 6 small meals, no fluids with meals, avoid simple sugars, more protein and fat, rest after eating

Scenario 4: TPN

  • Cues: Client in a UC flare, 8 kg weight loss, NPO for bowel rest; TPN started through a PICC
  • Analysis: Gut can't be used right now → TPN appropriate
  • Actions: Dedicated line; glucose checks about every 6 h; titrate up (and later down) slowly; plan to return to enteral feeding when the gut is ready
Priority cues across Week 6: board-like abdomen · rising HR with falling H&H · frank blood in emesis · restless patient · drinking before the gag reflex returns · stoma color change · no stool or gas for more than 24 hours after bowel surgery.

Self-Test Flashcards

Click a card to flip it and check your answer.

Which interventions share the goal of 'resting the gut'?
NPO with IV fluids during IBD flares, diverticulitis, and obstruction; NPO after EGD until the gag reflex returns.
Which Week 6 problems are treated with an NG tube to low wall suction?
PUD hemorrhage and obstruction, intestinal obstruction/paralytic ileus, and early after bariatric surgery.
Which conditions can cause a rigid, board-like abdomen?
Perforated ulcer, perforation from a GI bleed, ruptured diverticulum — all leading to peritonitis.
How can untreated GERD lead to cancer?
Chronic reflux → Barrett's esophagus (metaplasia) → 30–40× higher risk of esophageal cancer.
How does liver disease connect to an upper GI bleed?
Portal hypertension → esophageal varices, which can bleed uncontrollably (Blakemore tube, ICU).
Which surgeries can cause dumping syndrome?
Any that remove or bypass part of the stomach — gastrectomy/PUD surgery (Billroth), Roux-en-Y gastric bypass.
Which two conditions use opposite fiber advice?
Diverticulosis → high fiber. IBD after a flare → low residue.
Which condition has 'no special diet'?
Peptic ulcer disease — diet is individualized.
Why is ileostomy output liquid while sigmoid colostomy output is formed?
The colon reabsorbs water. An ileostomy exits before the colon; a sigmoid colostomy exits near its end.
Which Week 6 conditions raise colorectal cancer risk?
Inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's and UC), plus diet, obesity, inactivity, alcohol, smoking, and genetics.
A client with a GI problem becomes restless. What should the nurse do?
Investigate right away — restlessness can signal hypoxia, hypovolemia/shock (e.g., a GI bleed), or another serious change.
What single principle guides TPN vs tube feeding?
If the gut works, use it.
