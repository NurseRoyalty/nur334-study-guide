Polycythemia: Comprehensive Study Guide

Relative, Primary (Vera), and Secondary Polycythemia

📌 What IS Polycythemia?

Polycythemia = Too many RBCs; abnormally HIGH hematocrit and RBC count

Remember: Hematocrit is roughly 3x the hemoglobin value. In polycythemia, this ratio stays but values are HIGH.

The Core Problem: Thicker blood = decreased flow = tissue hypoxia and clotting risks

🔴 Types of Polycythemia

Type 1: RELATIVE POLYCYTHEMIA

Key Point: Due to dehydration (decreased plasma volume), NOT actual increase in RBC production
  • What's happening: Patient is dehydrated
  • Lab result: False HIGH hematocrit
  • Reality: No actual increase in RBC production
  • Example: Hematocrit goes from 45% to 52% overnight = patient probably dehydrated, not polycythemia
  • Nursing action: Rehydrate; don't treat as polycythemia

Type 2: PRIMARY POLYCYTHEMIA (Polycythemia Vera)

  • Cause: Genetic change during lifetime (not inherited)
  • Type: Myeloproliferative disorder
  • Prevention: NOT preventable
  • Duration: Chronic condition requiring ongoing management
  • Outlook: Lifelong treatment and monitoring needed

Type 3: SECONDARY POLYCYTHEMIA

Compensatory mechanism in response to tissue hypoxia
  • Most common cause: COPD/chronic hypoxia
  • Also seen in: Patients living at high altitude
  • Other causes: Chronic heart/lung disease, sleep apnea
  • Body's response: Makes more RBCs to compensate for low oxygen
  • Treatment: Treat the underlying hypoxia, not the polycythemia directly

⚠️ Clinical Manifestations: Blood Becomes THICKER

Cardiovascular Effects (From Increased Viscosity & Volume):

  • Hypertension: More blood volume = increased pressure
  • Headache: Blood congestion in head
  • Difficulty concentrating: Brain perfusion affected
  • Ruddy complexion: Dusky red color from excess RBCs
  • Possible cyanosis: Despite high RBCs (paradoxical)

Serious Complications from DECREASED Blood Flow:

  • Clot formation OR hemorrhage: Paradoxically both possible!
  • Decreased perfusion to heart: Angina, MI, TIA
  • MOST SERIOUS/COMMON ACUTE COMPLICATION: STROKE (thrombotic event)

Metabolic Effects:

  • Hypermetabolic state: High viscosity, high volume = increased metabolism
  • Night sweats: Profuse; may soak bedding
  • Weight loss: From high metabolism

UNIQUE to Polycythemia:

Pruritus (intense itching) worsened by hot baths = CLASSIC SIGN ON EXAMS

Also: Pain in fingers and toes

🔍 Nursing Assessment

  • Vital signs: Especially BP (often elevated)
  • Neurologic status: Critical - risk of stroke
  • Skin color and perfusion: Ruddy? Cyanotic?
  • Complaints of: Headache? Itching (pruritus)?
  • Activity tolerance: How tired are they?
  • Signs of bleeding or clotting (paradoxical risk)

💊 Nursing Interventions & Collaborative Care

PHLEBOTOMY: Mainstay of Treatment

Literally drain blood to decrease volume and viscosity
  • Initial schedule: 300-500 mL every other day
  • Maintenance: Eventually every few months
  • Goal: Keep hematocrit <42% in women, <45% in men
  • Nursing care: Monitor closely post-phlebotomy (BP, HR, dizziness)

Other Interventions:

  • Keep patient well-hydrated: Blood is viscous - needs fluids to thin it out
  • Anticoagulants: Due to clotting risk
  • Antihistamines: For severe itching (e.g., diphenhydramine)
  • Avoid prolonged immobility/bed rest: Paradoxically increases clot risk
  • Encourage mobility: Walking, regular activity
  • NOT preventable: Needs ongoing evaluation and support

❌ Complications (All Serious)

  • Stroke/TIA: From thrombotic events
  • MI/angina: From decreased coronary perfusion
  • Bleeding: Despite high RBC count (paradoxical)
  • Clot formation: In lungs, legs, brain
  • Acute leukemia: In some cases (long-term complication)

📚 Patient Education (Exam Focus)

  • Importance of hydration: Drink plenty of fluids
  • Avoid prolonged immobility: Get up, walk, move regularly
  • Report signs of stroke EARLY: Weakness on one side, speech changes, facial drooping
  • Report chest pain or angina: Don't ignore warning signs
  • Discuss phlebotomy schedule: Why we're draining blood (to decrease viscosity)
  • Avoid hot baths: Can worsen itching and increase vasodilation
  • Chronic condition requiring lifetime management: Not a one-time fix
  • Importance of follow-up appointments: Regular monitoring essential

📝 Key Takeaways

  • ✓ Three types: Relative (dehydration), Primary (Vera), Secondary (COPD/hypoxia)
  • ✓ Polycythemia = thick blood = clotting AND bleeding risk (paradoxical)
  • ✓ Pruritus worsened by hot baths = CLASSIC SIGN
  • ✓ Most serious acute complication = STROKE
  • ✓ Phlebotomy = mainstay of treatment (literally drain blood)
  • ✓ Asymptomatic doesn't mean safe - still has thrombotic risk
  • ✓ Nursing diagnoses: Risk for stroke, risk for bleeding, activity intolerance
  • ✓ Patient education about hydration, mobility, warning signs
