Disease Process Overview

HIV and AIDS Fundamentals

Definition and Stages

  • HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) attacks CD4+ T cells, which are crucial for coordinating immune responses
  • AIDS is the final stage of HIV infection, occurring when CD4 count drops below 200 cells/μL or when AIDS-defining opportunistic infections develop
  • Stage 1 (Acute Infection): Virus replicates rapidly but is undetectable on lab tests; no symptoms present but patient is highly infectious
  • Stage 2 (Seroconversion): Antibodies become detectable; patient feels extremely ill (worst flu ever); highly infectious period
  • Stage 3 (Clinical Latency): Can last 3-12+ years with no or mild symptoms; virus continues replicating; CD4 steadily drops
  • Stage 4 (AIDS): CD4 < 200 or opportunistic infections present; patient becomes severely immunocompromised

Transmission Routes

  • Sexual Contact: Anal or vaginal intercourse (primary mode of transmission)
  • Parenteral (Blood): Needle sharing among IV drug users; occupational needle sticks (0.3% risk even with HIV+ source)
  • Mother-to-Child: During pregnancy, labor, delivery, or breastfeeding
  • NOT Transmitted By: Casual contact, sharing food/drink, kissing, hugging, hand-holding, sharing toilets, or sharing office equipment
  • Bloodborne Fluid Exposure: Semen, vaginal secretions, breast milk, cerebrospinal fluid, synovial fluid, pleural fluid, peritoneal fluid, and pericardial fluid
  • Highest Risk Groups: Men who have sex with men (MSM), IV drug users, individuals who exchange sex for money/drugs, sex partners of HIV+ individuals

Integumentary Manifestations

Skin Conditions Associated with HIV

  • Kaposi Sarcoma (KS): Purple/brown non-blanching painless lesions; caused by HHV-8; appears when CD4 < 50; highly associated with advanced AIDS; can metastasize to any organ
  • Oral Candidiasis (Thrush): White patches in mouth; occurs when CD4 < 200; indicates risk for esophageal involvement; requires antifungal therapy
  • Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV): Recurrent or severe vesicles; can be severe in immunocompromised states; distinguish from VZV by dermatomal distribution
  • Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV/Shingles): Vesicles in dermatomal distribution; can be severe with low CD4; dermatomal pattern helps differentiate from HSV
  • Molluscum Contagiosum: Umbilicated lesions; more common, numerous, and severe when CD4 < 100; may improve with ART
  • Seborrheic Dermatitis: Greasy yellowish scales on face/scalp; fungal (Malassezia) condition; worse with stress and poor hygiene
  • Oral Manifestations: Extremely common with advancing HIV; includes thrush, herpes, aphthous ulcers, warts

Skin Cancer Types and Risk

Non-Melanoma and Melanoma Classification

  • Basal Cell Carcinoma: Locally invasive; red pearly lesions with defined borders; barely elevated plaques; may have central depression; most common
  • Squamous Cell Carcinoma: From keratinized epidermal cells; aggressive; presents with scaling; can metastasize
  • Malignant Melanoma: From melanocytes; genetic and environmental factors; poor prognosis unless diagnosed early; may metastasize to any organ
  • ABCDE Rule for Melanoma: Asymmetry (not symmetrical), Border irregularity (ragged/notched), Color variation (multiple colors), Diameter (>6mm), Evolving (changing)
  • Risk Factors for Skin Cancer: Fair skin, blonde/red hair, blue eyes, family/personal history, outdoor occupation, high altitude/equator living, indoor tanning

  • Each leg: 18% (9% anterior + 9% posterior)
  • Each arm: 9% (4.5% anterior + 4.5% posterior)
  • Trunk anterior: 18%
  • Trunk posterior: 18%
  • Head/Neck: 9% (4.5% face + 4.5% posterior)
  • Genitalia: 1%
  • Total: 100%

Self-Test Flashcards

Test your understanding of comprehensive Week 5 material. Click cards to flip.

What is the difference between HIV and AIDS?
HIV is the virus; AIDS is the final stage when CD4 < 200 or AIDS-defining OIs develop. HIV progresses to AIDS over years without treatment.
List the four stages of HIV infection.
1) Acute infection (rapid replication, undetectable), 2) Seroconversion (antibodies detectable, worst flu), 3) Clinical latency (asymptomatic, 3-12+ years), 4) AIDS (CD4 < 200, severe immunosuppression).
What is the significance of CD4 count of 200?
CD4 < 200 indicates AIDS diagnosis. Patient becomes vulnerable to opportunistic infections like PCP, toxoplasmosis, CMV, and cryptococcal meningitis. Prophylaxis is indicated.
How is HIV transmitted?
Sexual contact (anal/vaginal), parenteral (needle sharing, occupational exposure), mother-to-child (pregnancy/delivery/breastfeeding). NOT transmitted by casual contact.
What is the needle stick risk for HIV transmission from HIV+ source?
Approximately 0.3% (3 in 1000). Very low but requires post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) within 1-2 hours, ideally within 3 days.
Name three AIDS-defining opportunistic infections.
Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP), toxoplasmosis, CMV, cryptococcal meningitis, tuberculosis, oral candidiasis, Kaposi Sarcoma, MAC (mycobacterium avium).
What causes Kaposi Sarcoma and when does it appear?
HHV-8 (human herpesvirus 8) causes KS. Appears when CD4 < 50. Presents as purple/brown non-blanching painless lesions that can occur anywhere and metastasize.
How does oral candidiasis differ from oral herpes in HIV?
Candidiasis = white patches, no dermatomal pattern, occurs at CD4 < 200; HSV = painful vesicles, can appear anywhere, severe with low CD4. VZV = dermatomal distribution (shingles pattern).
What is the ABCDE rule for detecting melanoma?
A=Asymmetry, B=Border irregularity (ragged/notched), C=Color variation (multiple colors), D=Diameter >6mm, E=Evolving (changing). Any present = refer for biopsy.
What are the three phases of wound healing?
1) Hemostasis/Inflammatory (0-4 days) = clotting, inflammation; 2) Proliferative (4-21 days) = collagen, angiogenesis, granulation tissue; 3) Maturation (21 days-2 years) = remodeling, scarring.
Distinguish between partial-thickness and full-thickness burns.
Partial-thickness = epidermis + dermis, blistered, pink, very painful, heals in 2-3 weeks; Full-thickness = all layers, charred/black, painless (nerves destroyed), requires grafting.
Calculate body surface area for a 70kg patient with 40% BSA burn using Rule of 9s.
40% BSA means 40% of total body surface is burned. This determines severity (moderate-severe) and guides Parkland formula: 4 mL × 70 kg × 40% = 11,200 mL LR in 24 hours.
