Diagnostic Algorithm and Interpretation

HIV Diagnosis Confirmation Process

  • Step 1: Initial Screening: 4th generation (Ag/Ab) or 3rd generation test; also acceptable: rapid test or NAT
  • Step 2: Confirm Positive (if 4th/3rd gen positive): HIV-1/HIV-2 differentiation immunoassay to distinguish between HIV-1 and HIV-2
  • Step 3: Final Confirmation: HIV-1 nucleic acid test (NAT/RNA/viral load) to confirm infection and detect acute infection with negative/indeterminate antibody tests
  • Special Case - Acute Infection: Patient has high viral load and p24 antigen but negative antibodies; NAT is ONLY way to detect; can transmit despite negative standard antibody tests
  • Negative Result Interpretation: If appropriate window period has passed (>45 days since exposure), negative result reliably excludes HIV; if within window period, retest in 1-2 weeks

CD4 Percentage vs Absolute Count

  • Absolute CD4 Count: Actual number of CD4+ T cells per microliter (normal 500-1200); most important for disease staging and OI prophylaxis decisions
  • CD4 Percentage: Percentage of total lymphocytes that are CD4+; less variable than absolute count but less predictive for OI risk; useful in children where counts vary by age
  • Clinical Use: Use absolute count for staging, prophylaxis decisions, and OI risk assessment; monitor trends over time (CD4 velocity)
  • Immune Reconstitution: On ART, CD4 increases 50-100 cells/month in first year; slower later; goal is CD4 > 200-500 depending on OI risk

Viral Load Testing and Disease Progression

Viral Load (HIV RNA) Monitoring

RNA Level Interpretation and Prognosis

  • Viral Load Measurement: Copies of HIV RNA per milliliter of blood; measured by RT-PCR (reverse transcriptase PCR)
  • Set Point (Viral Set Point): Each person has a characteristic baseline viral load; genetic factors determine set point; predicts disease progression
  • Acute Infection (Seroconversion): Viral load spikes to millions of copies (100,000 to millions); then drops as antibodies appear; set point established by ~6 months
  • Chronic Phase: Viral load relatively stable at individual's set point; virus actively replicating; CD4 steadily declining over years (200-300 cells/year on average without treatment)
  • Clinical Utility: Baseline viral load predicts progression (higher = faster progression); monitored to assess ART effectiveness (should drop >1 log within 4 weeks, undetectable <50 copies by 6 months); helps guide prophylaxis decisions
  • Undetectable = Untransmittable (U=U): When viral load <50 copies/mL on ART, transmission via sexual contact is prevented (no transmission risk documented)

Combination Therapy and Treatment Regimens

\n
\n

Standard First-Line Regimens

\n
    \n
  • Recommended Combo Approach: Integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI) + 2 NRTIs OR INSTI monotherapy/dual therapy in selected patients
    • \n
  • Preferred First-Line (2024): Dolutegravir + tenofovir/emtricitabine (Bictarvy, Triumeq, Dovato); or bictegravir/TAF/FTC single tablet daily
    • \n
  • Alternative Regimens: Efavirenz/TDF/FTC (older, more side effects); PI-based (darunavir/ritonavir + 2 NRTIs) for treatment-experienced
    • \n
  • When to Start ART: ALL HIV+ patients should start ART regardless of CD4 count (immediate treatment = Start Antiretroviral Therapy = START)
    • \n
  • Timing: Start ASAP after diagnosis; CD4 < 200 is urgent (within 2 weeks); CD4 > 200 can wait 1-2 weeks for baseline labs and education
    • \n
  • Adherence Critical: >95% adherence needed to prevent resistance; missing doses allows viral replication and mutations; counsel extensively
    • \n

ART Toxicity and Monitoring

\n
\n

Common Side Effects and Management

\n
    \n
  • Efavirenz (NNRTI): CNS effects (dizziness, vivid dreams, depression, suicide risk) in 50%; take at night; counsel on safety driving; teratogenic
    • \n
  • Tenofovir (NRTI): Kidney toxicity (monitor creatinine); bone loss (osteoporosis risk); TAF (tenofovir alafenamide) = newer formulation with less kidney/bone toxicity
    • \n
  • Integrase Inhibitors (INSTI): Weight gain (especially dolutegravir 2-3 kg); hyperglycemia possible; rash (bictegravir rare); generally very well-tolerated
    • \n
  • Protease Inhibitors (PI): GI upset, diarrhea; hyperglycemia; dyslipidemia (lipid elevation); lipodystrophy (fat redistribution); drug interactions
    • \n
  • Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS): Paradoxical worsening of OI symptoms when CD4 rises on ART; immune system \"wakes up\"; manage with NSAIDs/corticosteroids; don't stop ART
    • \n
  • Baseline Labs Before Starting: CD4, viral load, genotype (resistance testing), CBC, CMP, lipid panel, kidney/liver function, TB screening, Hep B/C, RPR (syphilis)
    • \n
  • Monitoring During ART: CD4 and viral load at 4 weeks, 12 weeks, then every 3-6 months; CBC, CMP at 4 weeks then per protocol; lipid panel yearly
    • \n

Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)

\n
\n

Emergency Prevention After Exposure

\n
    \n
  • Indications: Occupational needlestick (0.3% transmission risk), unprotected sexual contact with HIV+ partner, IV drug needle sharing, perinatal exposure
    • \n
  • Timing Critical: MUST start within 1-2 hours (absolute maximum 72 hours); most effective if started immediately; benefit disappears after 72 hours
    • \n
  • Regimen: 28-day course of 3-drug ART combination (typically TDF/FTC + PI like darunavir or INSTI like dolutegravir); same drugs used for treatment-naïve
    • \n
  • Counseling: Adherence critical for efficacy; side effects common; discuss safer sex/needle practices; condom use during PEP; anxiety support
    • \n
  • Follow-up Testing: Baseline HIV test (confirm negative before starting), repeat at 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months post-exposure (window period precautions)
    • \n
  • Healthcare Worker Exposure: Report immediately; occupational health/ID consultation; insurance coverage; worker's comp; document thoroughly
    • \n

Patient Education and Psychosocial Support

\n

Key Education Topics for HIV+ Patients

\n
\n

Empowerment and Self-Management

\n
    \n
  • Transmission Prevention: U=U (undetectable = untransmittable); condom use even on ART (STI prevention); disclosure to partners; safe sex negotiation
    • \n
  • Medication Adherence: >95% adherence critical; pill management strategies; reminder systems; side effect management; what to do if doses missed
    • \n
  • Opportunistic Infection Symptoms: Teach red flags (fever, SOB, night sweats, cough, oral thrush, vision changes); when to seek care immediately
    • \n
  • Lifestyle Factors: Nutrition and immune function; importance of routine; vaccinations (flu, pneumococcal, HPV, meningococcal); smoking/substance abuse cessation; exercise
    • \n
  • Mental Health: Screen for depression, anxiety, PTSD; connect to counseling; normalize emotional responses to diagnosis; support groups; peer mentorship
    • \n
  • Sexual Health: Reproductive choices; fertility in HIV+ persons (treatment protects fertility); safe conception strategies for serodiscordant couples
    • \n
  • Disclosure Strategies: To whom, when, how; partner communication; managing stigma; family dynamics; workplace disclosure (not legally required but impacts support)
    • \n

\n
