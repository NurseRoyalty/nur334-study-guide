Hematology Labs & Diagnostic Tests

Complete Blood Count, Iron Studies, B12/Folate Tests, and Specialized Diagnostics

🎯 Key Concepts

RBC Bus Analogy: Think of RBCs as buses that transport oxygen. Hemoglobin represents the seats in the bus. Iron is what the seats are made of. Oxygen is the passenger. This helps you understand what's happening when values are abnormal.

Hematocrit (Hct): Percentage of blood volume made up of RBCs

  • Normal: 37-47% in women; 42-52% in men
  • Roughly 3x the hemoglobin value
  • MEMORIZE these normal ranges for exam

Hemoglobin (Hb): Iron-containing pigment of RBCs; carries oxygen

  • Normal: Women 12-16 g/dL; Men 14-18 g/dL
  • CRITICAL TO KNOW - this appears on every exam
  • Roughly 1/3 the hematocrit value

Complete Blood Count (CBC): Assesses RBCs, hemoglobin, hematocrit, WBC count, differential, and platelets in one test

O2 Saturation (SpO2): How full the "buses" (RBCs) are carrying oxygen

  • Want: ≥95% (at least 95% of RBCs loaded with oxygen)
  • Below 90% = concerning; requires intervention

📊 Complete Blood Count (CBC) Components

RBC Tests & Morphology

RBC Count: Rarely used clinically; H&H (hemoglobin & hematocrit) used much more commonly

RBC Morphology: Describes color, size, and shape of cells. Critical for diagnosing different types of anemia.

Test What It Measures Normal/Interpretations
MCV
(Mean Corpuscular Volume)		 Size of RBC • Normalcytic = normal size
• Microcytic = small (e.g., iron deficiency)
• Macrocytic = large (e.g., B12/folate deficiency)
MCHC
(Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration)		 Hemoglobin concentration in cells • Normalchromic = normal concentration
• Hypochromic = low concentration (pale cells) = iron deficiency

WBC Tests & Differential

Normal WBC: 5,000-10,000/μL (memorize this!)

Differential Count: Determines the percentage of each WBC type present

Never Let Monkeys Eat Bananas = Neutrophils, Lymphocytes, Monocytes, Eosinophils, Basophils
  • Neutrophils: Most plentiful WBC (~60-70%); first responders to infection
  • Increase with: Acute bacterial infection
  • "Shift to the Left": Release of immature neutrophils (bands) indicates onset of ACUTE BACTERIAL INFECTION - RED FLAG for examiners

Platelet Count

Normal: 150,000-400,000/μL
  • Below 150,000 = thrombocytopenia (bleeding risk)
  • Above 400,000 = thrombocytosis (clotting risk)

📈 Trending Labs - Think Trends, NOT Standalone Values

CRITICAL CONCEPT FOR NCLEX: Always compare lab values to baseline and previous values. A single value tells you nothing. Trends tell you everything.

Example: H&H of 9.2/28.1 might look concerning by itself, BUT if that value is trending UPWARD from the previous day (9.0/27.8), you keep monitoring. If it's trending DOWN, that's concerning.

This is why you MUST know:

  • Patient's baseline (what's normal for THEM?)
  • Previous lab values (yesterday, last week)
  • Direction of trend (up or down?)
  • Rate of change (fast drop vs. slow decline?)

⚠️ False Lab Values - Not What They Appear

False Low Hematocrit

  • What looks wrong: Hematocrit is low
  • Actual problem: Patient has fluid retention (heart failure, kidney failure)
  • Reality: Actual RBC numbers are NORMAL - just diluted by excess fluid
  • Action: Treat the underlying fluid problem, not anemia

False High Hematocrit

  • What looks wrong: Hematocrit is elevated
  • Actual problem: Patient is dehydrated
  • Reality: Increases percentage but no true increase in RBCs - just concentrated blood
  • Action: Rehydrate; don't treat as polycythemia
These false values cause unnecessary treatment if you don't consider the clinical picture. Always ask: "What else could cause this lab value?"

🔬 Iron Studies - Key Tests for Iron Deficiency Diagnosis

Test What It Measures In Iron Deficiency Sensitivity
Serum Iron Amount of iron in blood LOW Moderate
Transferrin Protein that TRansports iron DECREASED Moderate
Ferritin Protein that stores iron (iron reserve) DECREASED MOST SENSITIVE
Memory Trick: Transferrin TRansports; Ferritin Stores. This helps you remember which protein does what.

🧪 Other Diagnostic Tests

Antibodies to Intrinsic Factor

  • What it assesses: Presence of antibodies attacking intrinsic factor
  • Intrinsic factor: Produced by parietal cells in stomach; REQUIRED for B12 absorption
  • If positive: Pernicious anemia (autoimmune condition)

Serum B12 and Serum Folate

  • When ordered: Only when anemia of unknown origin suspected
  • What they measure: Presence of these vitamin levels
  • Low levels: Indicate deficiency

Coombs Test (Antiglobulin Test)

Type What It Tests When Used
Direct Coombs Tests for incompatible blood transfusion (wrong blood given to patient) After transfusion reaction; differentiate hemolytic anemia types
Indirect Coombs Screens for compatible blood BEFORE transfusion Pre-transfusion screening in blood bank

Stool Testing for Occult Blood (Guaiac)

  • Purpose: Tests for hidden blood in stool
  • When used: Anemia of unknown origin suspected; GI bleed suspected
  • Frank blood: Visible blood = obvious GI problem
  • Melena: Dark, tarry, malodorous stool (digested blood in GI tract)
  • Guaiac positive: Blood present but NOT visible to naked eye
Clinical Pearl: When anemia is of unknown cause, the FIRST place to suspect bleeding is the GI tract. That's why occult blood testing is often the first step.

Upper & Lower GI Endoscopy

  • EGD (Esophagogastroduodenoscopy): Scopes esophagus, stomach, duodenum; goes down throat
  • Colonoscopy: Scopes colon; goes in rectum
  • Purpose: Visual inspection to find source of GI bleed
  • Advantage: Can biopsy or treat bleeding source during procedure

Bone Marrow Biopsy

  • Type: Invasive procedure to assess blood-forming tissue in bone marrow
  • When indicated: Low RBCs, platelets, WBCs with UNKNOWN cause
  • Most common indication: Aplastic anemia diagnosis
  • Sites: Usually posterior iliac crest or anterior sternum

Nursing Implications:

  • Informed consent required (invasive procedure)
  • Timeout procedure (safety verification)
  • May give pre-medication for anxiety
  • Position patient correctly
  • Post-procedure: Gentle pressure to site; assess for bleeding
  • Monitor for infection at biopsy site
  • Reassure patient - brief procedure but can be uncomfortable
