Postoperative Nursing

Content Status: This page covers postoperative care from recovery room through discharge — vital sign monitoring, pain management, complications (DVT, PE, infection, ileus), wound care, and patient education.

Immediate Postoperative Period (PACU/Phase 1)

  • Aldrete score: Assesses recovery from anesthesia. Measures consciousness, airway patency, respiration, circulation, SpO₂, temperature, activity, pain, nausea/vomiting. Score ≥9 = ready for discharge to floor.
  • Initial assessment: Airway open? Breathing adequate? Circulation stable? Responsive? Bleeding controlled?
  • Positioning: Lateral or semi-Fowler to prevent aspiration. Keep side rails up, call light accessible. NPO until fully alert.
  • Monitoring: Vitals q5-15min, SpO₂ continuous, pain level, surgical site, drains, catheters, IV lines.

Postoperative Pain Management

PAIN ASSESSMENT: Character, onset, location, duration, severity (0–10 scale), aggravating/alleviating factors, effect on function.
  • Opioids: First-line for moderate-severe pain. Examples: morphine, hydromorphone, oxycodone. Monitor for respiratory depression, constipation, sedation.
  • Non-opioids: Acetaminophen, NSAIDs (if no contraindications). Multimodal analgesia = opioid + non-opioid + adjuvants (regional block, gabapentin) reduces opioid requirements.
  • Regional anesthesia: Peripheral nerve blocks, epidural catheters provide pain relief with less opioid use.
  • Non-pharmacological: Splinting incision, ice/heat, positioning, distraction, relaxation techniques.
  • Goal: Pain control that allows movement, coughing, and early mobilization to prevent complications.

Wound Care & Healing

  • Phases of healing: Inflammatory (0–3 days), proliferative (3–21 days), remodeling (weeks to months).
  • Dressing changes: Follow protocol. Signs of infection: purulent drainage, redness, warmth, swelling, foul odor, fever.
  • Suture/staple removal: Typically 7–10 days (face) to 14 days (body). Leave in longer if patient on steroids or immunocompromised.
  • Drain management: Empty drains q4–8h, measure output (serosanguineous, serous, seropurulent), document color/volume, keep below wound level, ensure tubing not kinked.
  • Activity: Gradual return to normal. Avoid strenuous activity/lifting until cleared by surgeon (typically 4–6 weeks).

Discharge Planning & Home Care

  • Medications: Review all medications, especially pain meds, antibiotics, DVT prophylaxis. Provide written list.
  • Diet: Progress as tolerated. If GI surgery, provide specific restrictions.
  • Activity restrictions: Lifting limits, driving restrictions (while on opioids), return-to-work timeline.
  • Wound care: Keep clean and dry. When to shower (usually when incision closed/staples removed). Signs to report: increasing redness, drainage, separation, fever.
  • Follow-up appointments: Ensure patient has surgeon's phone number, post-op visit scheduled (usually 1–2 weeks).
  • Signs to report: Fever >101.5°F, severe pain unrelieved by meds, chest pain, dyspnea, calf swelling, wound issues.

Study Flashcards

What is the Aldrete score used for? What score indicates readiness for discharge from PACU?
Aldrete score assesses recovery from anesthesia (consciousness, airway, respiration, circulation, SpO₂, temp, activity, pain, nausea). Score ≥9 indicates readiness to discharge to floor.
A post-op patient on day 3 has fever (38.5°C), calf swelling, and mild chest pain. Suspect complication?
DVT with possible PE. Immobility post-op is a risk factor. Notify surgeon, get duplex ultrasound/D-dimer, start anticoagulation, encourage ambulation.
A post-op patient has absent bowel sounds, distension, and nausea on day 2. What's the likely issue and treatment?
Postoperative ileus. Keep NPO, consider NG tube if severe, maintain IV hydration, encourage early ambulation, stimulate bowel with walking and position changes.
When are surgical sutures/staples typically removed?
7–10 days for face (better cosmetics); 14 days for body. Delay removal in patients on steroids or who are immunocompromised.
When can a post-op patient typically return to work and strenuous activities?
Usually 4–6 weeks depending on surgery type and healing. Follow surgeon's clearance. Must avoid lifting and strenuous activity during recovery.
