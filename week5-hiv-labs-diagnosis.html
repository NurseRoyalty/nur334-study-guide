HIV Labs & Diagnosis

Comprehensive coverage of HIV testing, laboratory monitoring, and diagnostic criteria for managing HIV-positive patients.

Screening Recommendations & Testing Guidelines

CDC Testing Recommendations

Universal and Risk-Based Screening

  • Universal Screening: CDC recommends all adults 13-64 get tested at least once as part of routine medical care
  • Annual Testing: Recommended for sexually active persons not in mutually monogamous relationships
  • Risk-Based Screening: Persons with multiple or anonymous sexual partners, those with STI history, MSM, transgender persons, persons with injection drug use history, incarcerated persons, sex workers, clients of sex workers
  • Pregnant Women: All pregnant women should be screened as early as possible in pregnancy; repeat at third trimester if initially negative
  • Occupational Exposure: Healthcare workers after needlestick or sharps injuries

Fourth Generation Testing (Recommended)

Fourth Generation Test Characteristics

Test FeatureDetails
What It DetectsAntibodies to HIV AND p24 antigen (core protein of HIV); detects both immune response AND virus
Sensitivity99% sensitive; detects infection earlier than 3rd gen (antibody-only tests)
Specificity99% specific; very few false positives
Window Period18-45 days average (compared to 23-35 days for 3rd gen); p24 antigen detectable before antibodies
Test Types AvailableBlood test (most reliable), oral fluid test, rapid testing (20 minutes)
False PositivesRare; any positive result requires confirmation with Western blot or HIV-1/HIV-2 differentiation immunoassay

Window Period & Eclipse Period Concepts

  • Window Period (Diagnostic Window): Time between HIV infection and when tests become positive; ranges 18-45 days for 4th generation tests
  • During Window Period: Person is acutely infected, highly infectious, but test is negative; most dangerous transmission period
  • Eclipse Period: Very early infection when viral load is highest but neither p24 antigen nor antibodies are detectable; typically only 3-5 days but most infectious period
  • Clinical Implications: Persons with negative test but recent exposure (within 72 hours) should start post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) immediately; do not wait for confirmation
  • Repeat Testing: If negative test but high-risk exposure, repeat at 1 month (covers most window periods) and 3 months to fully exclude infection

CD4 Percentage vs Absolute Count

Understanding CD4 Percentage

  • CD4 Percentage: Proportion of lymphocytes that are CD4+ cells; normal range 30-60%; more stable than absolute count
  • Absolute Count vs Percentage: Absolute count reflects true CD4 status in severe immunosuppression; use percentage if absolute count unreliable (hemolysis, technical issues)
  • AIDS Diagnosis by CD4%: CD4% < 14% is equivalent to CD4 count < 200 cells/μL
  • Clinical Use: Monitor both; percentage helps identify discordant results that may indicate need for retesting

Viral Load Testing & Treatment Goals

Viral Load Monitoring

Viral Load MeasureDetails
What It MeasuresQuantitative PCR (qPCR) measuring HIV RNA copies per milliliter of plasma; indicates viral replication rate

U=U Principle (Undetectable = Untransmittable)

  • The U=U Statement: People with HIV on effective ART with undetectable viral loads cannot sexually transmit HIV
  • Evidence Base: Multiple large studies (PARTNER, Opposites Attract studies) demonstrate zero transmission with undetectable status
  • Maintained Undetectability: Requires adherence to ART; viral rebound can occur if treatment interrupted
  • Clinical Significance: Eliminates treatment-as-prevention barrier for reproduction; reduces stigma; guides sexual health counseling
  • Nursing Role: Educate patients on U=U principle; emphasize importance of maintaining undetectable status for themselves AND their partners

Additional Monitoring Laboratories

Baseline Labs at Diagnosis

Essential Initial Laboratory Testing

  • Complete Blood Count (CBC): Assesses for anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia; baseline for monitoring ART effects
  • Liver Function Tests (LFTs): AST, ALT, bilirubin, albumin; establishes baseline; critical for some ART drugs (hepatotoxicity risk)
  • Renal Function: BUN, creatinine, creatinine clearance; important for dosing certain ART medications (TDF, PI-based regimens)
  • Lipid Panel: Cholesterol, triglycerides; baseline for protease inhibitor side effects
  • Blood Glucose: Screen for diabetes; important as ART can affect glucose metabolism
  • Genotype Resistance Testing: Performed at baseline to identify drug-resistant strains and guide ART selection
  • STI Screening: Syphilis, gonorrhea, chlamydia, hepatitis B and C, HSV serology
  • TB Screening: Tuberculin skin test or IGRA; critical as TB coinfection is common

Ongoing Monitoring with ART

Regular Lab Monitoring Schedule

  • Viral Load & CD4: 2-4 weeks after starting ART (assess initial response), then every 3 months initially, then every 6-12 months if stable
  • CBC, LFTs, Renal: Baseline, then 2-4 weeks after ART initiation, then every 3-6 months depending on ART regimen
  • Lipid Panel: Baseline, then 4-8 weeks after starting lipid-affecting drugs, then annually
  • Glucose Monitoring: Baseline, then periodic screening; consider hemoglobin A1C if elevated glucose
  • Drug Level Monitoring: Some PI-based regimens require therapeutic drug monitoring
  • Resistance Testing: Repeat if viral load rebounds despite adherence (confirms resistance development)

OI Screening by CD4 Count

  • TB Screening (All CD4): IGRA preferred over TST; repeat annually if negative and CD4 < 100
  • MAC Prophylaxis Indication: CD4 < 50; azithromycin weekly; stop when CD4 > 50 for 3+ months on ART
  • CMV Monitoring: CD4 < 50; baseline retinal exam; dilated exam every 1-3 months; symptoms warrant urgent ophthalmology referral
  • Cryptococcal Antigen (CrAg): Screen when CD4 < 100; positive result indicates need for antifungal prophylaxis
  • Toxoplasmosis Prevention: CD4 < 100 with positive IgG serology; requires TMP-SMX (covers both toxo and PCP)
